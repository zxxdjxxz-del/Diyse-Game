## Cyanis character audit — COMPLETE

The Chapter-0 atomic dialogue has been checked specifically against current Cyanis character authority rather than assuming the broader Person-Brain pass covered every possible drift.

### Audit target
Cyanis must not collapse into:
- generic protagonist commander;
- exposition-question feed for more specialized characters;
- infallible decision-maker;
- constant martyr with no ordinary personality;
- person who owns every plan merely because he is the controllable lead;
- character whose humor disappears whenever plot information enters the scene.

Current active character rule:
> **Leadership is not a dialogue assignment.**

Cyanis may ask a question when he genuinely wants the answer, but he is not required to be the automatic prompt that lets another character explain the route, Card, medicine, politics, evidence, or next objective.

### P01 — Convoy / Opening Ambush
- Cyanis enters through competence, mild insubordination, dry humor, and practical attention rather than protagonist grandeur.
- he notices the empty-road problem and explains the reasoning behind his concern.
- the ranking convoy officer does not surrender authority merely because Cyanis is right to raise the concern.
- Cyanis can joke with an officer who already knows his habits.

### P02 — Wreck Field
- Cyanis gives practical rescue instructions without becoming a medical specialist.
- he can make a bounded field judgment under uncertainty rather than either knowing the north cut is bait or obediently accepting the first interpretation.
- `Mine` / `I noticed` / the `mostly` callback preserve personality inside crisis work.
- he does not solve the entire field alone; escorts and civilians retain agency and work.

### P03 — Evacuation Relay Decision
- Cyanis gives a competent complete field report rather than functioning as a checklist-answer machine.
- he argues against a chase with actual tradeoffs rather than heroic certainty.
- the officer identifies Cyanis's self-expendability pattern and keeps final command authority.
- Cyanis is capable of saying `You're right` without a defensive speech.

### P04 — Field Triage / Ilyra / First Flare
- Cyanis and Ilyra meet as professionals solving overlapping problems.
- he initially minimizes his own symptoms, then improves the information when Ilyra explains why precision matters.
- he accepts Ilyra's medical expertise without giving up operational judgment or turning her into his caretaker.
- his humor and mild embarrassment remain present even while the unexplained Card event makes him the medical subject.

### P05 — Concealed Ruin Vanguard
- Cyanis makes a tactical judgment about not being pulled away from camp rather than being assigned a generic leader speech.
- he is curious about the Vanguard's motive because **he** wants to understand what happened, not merely to feed Ilyra an exposition prompt.
- he takes water without being managed and volunteers his own symptom update from established continuity.
- the reset scene proves he can participate in an ordinary, uncertain conversation rather than only directing action.

### P06 — Riftmaw + War-Sorcerer
- high danger appropriately compresses his speech into real command language.
- his double-vision joke is a believable bad joke under stress, and he accepts Ilyra's correction when she explains why it corrupts medical information.
- he apologizes plainly and gives better information rather than performing vulnerability for the scene.
- he does not attempt to explain or classify the Card just because he is the protagonist.

### P07 — Aftermath / Survivor Recovery
- Cyanis argues for one bounded survivor sweep because the immediate human consequence matters to him, not because he must solve every missing-person problem indefinitely.
- he accepts the officer's boundary and does not turn the sweep into pursuit.
- he does not interpret Ilyra staying as devotion or debt; when corrected, he accepts her professional reason.
- his concern about whether Ilyra's patients are covered is consistent with his responsibility pattern without becoming ownership of her decision.

### C01 — Six Minutes
- this remains the strongest Chapter-0 proof that Cyanis is pleasant company rather than merely useful company.
- he starts and sustains jokes, argues over shared blame, laughs at Ilyra's bad joke, admits an embarrassing habit, notices her `find a task` posture because he recognizes the same behavior in himself, and invents the arbitrary six-minute rule.
- the scene contains no plot-question function for him at all.
- his ordinary personality survives when command pressure disappears.

The existing current scenes already satisfy his character authority. The audit instead adds a forward guardrail: future dialogue must preserve Cyanis as a social person with opinions, humor, wants, mistakes, and ordinary interests rather than using him mainly as the leader who asks the next useful question.

## Person-Brain performance pass — COMPLETE

This pass supersedes the earlier assumption that crisis dialogue should remain clipped merely because the scene is under pressure.

Current rule:
- true commands, immediate danger calls, and battle warnings may remain extremely short;
- field reports, tactical judgments, treatment reasoning, disagreements, and quiet scenes may hold the floor for as long as the person and situation require;
- interruptions must come from motive, urgency, expertise, humor, or relationship rather than from an artificial sentence-length ceiling;
- different speakers keep different rhythms;
- longer speech may not be used to repeat information the player has just seen.

### P01 — Convoy / Opening Ambush
- Cyanis and the convoy officer sound familiar with each other's habits before the attack;
- Cyanis explains why the empty road bothers him rather than supplying only a clue fragment;
- once the ambush begins, language appropriately collapses back into commands.

### P02 — Wreck Field
- rescue exchanges give civilians/escorts enough reasoning to act without turning Cyanis into a medical specialist;
- the north-cut discussion becomes an actual disagreement about uncertainty and pursuit rather than one-clause question/answer ping-pong;
- the officer asks Cyanis to bring a broader field picture before the next decision.

### P03 — Evacuation Relay Decision

- the prior spoken-dialogue correction remains intact: `You said that. What changes now?` turns the argument toward the five people who still need carrying rather than repeating the north-cut evidence;
- the officer challenges Cyanis's habit of objecting to needless exposure and then volunteering himself for the dangerous option;
- formal authority remains with the officer.

### P04 — Field Triage / Ilyra / First Flare

### P05 — Concealed Ruin Vanguard
