## [GAMEPLAY — ARRIVAL AT THE JUNCTION]

Player control is active.

The route rises out of Southern Briar into the old crossroads Torren knows as **the Junction**.

There are no random encounters here.

Chapter 1's combat climax is over.

The Junction itself is familiar ground to Torren: several old routes once converged here, and the modern southern road still passes through the same terrain.

The ancient structure shown in the Hollow Watch landscape depiction is not immediately recognizable.

## [STORY TRIGGER — THE HIDDEN MONUMENT]

The party reaches the center of the crossroads.

**TORREN:** This is it.

Cyanis looks around.

**CYANIS:** Then where's the thing from the mural?

**TORREN:** That's what I'm trying to figure out.

Maevra looks across the center of the Junction.

**MAEVRA:** It was in the middle.

Torren's attention settles on a broad, low mass of old fitted stone almost completely swallowed by vines and moss.

A beat.

**TORREN:** ...Shit.

Cyanis follows his look.

**CYANIS:** That?

**TORREN:** Maybe.

**MAEVRA:** You've walked past it before.

**TORREN:** I've walked past a lot of rocks.

**MAEVRA:** I'm keeping that answer.

**TORREN:** Of course you are.

The party clears the vegetation from the central stone.

This is one simple major-story interaction/state change rather than a long animation.

Beneath the vines and moss is a broad stone-and-metal cartographic surface.

The humor stops.

A beat.

**TORREN:** That's not a foundation.

Torren studies it.

**TORREN:** Route map, I think. Or close enough I'm willing to call it one. I know the ground; I don't know this system.

## [THE WAYFINDER]

The exposed surface is much larger in scope than a local route plate.

Once the surviving relationships resolve, it reads as a broad map of **western ancient Diyse**.

Dense overland route lines intersect and reconnect across the entire surviving region.

A second, visually distinct system of markings threads through the map in a different grammar.

**Production authority:** those markings correspond to underground infrastructure and routes, but Chapter 1 must not visually or verbally identify them that way yet. The party only recognizes a separate large-scale system distinct from ordinary surface roads.

Repeated built-form clusters mark numerous ancient cities or settlements.

Six familiar Face markings appear as part of the old technical grammar:

**Might. Elements. Grace. Memory. Perception. Ruin.**

Their function here is not understood.

Torren studies the surface longer.

**TORREN:** This isn't a Junction map.

Ilyra follows the route layers outward.

**ILYRA:** No.

Torren traces one set, then another without touching the surface.

**TORREN:** These are roads. These other lines aren't—or not any kind I know. They're everywhere, and I can't tell you what they mean.

Maevra looks across the repeated built-up clusters.

**MAEVRA:** And all of those?

**TORREN:** Built places. Settlements. Cities, maybe. A lot of them.

Torren comes back to the local ground first and follows a surviving alignment running southeast.

**TORREN:** This southeast line matches our road. Same direction, same bend. Could be the old road under parts of it, could just be people choosing the same good ground. Can't tell from this.

**MAEVRA:** Dunmere.

**TORREN:** Yeah.

Cyanis looks across the wider map.

**CYANIS:** So this whole thing was connected.

**TORREN:** Looks that way. Doesn't tell us how it all worked.

Farther across the surviving western map, surface routes, the second unidentified marking system, and built-up structures become markedly denser around one enormous concentration.

The physical map is broken through the middle of it.

Maevra compares the relationship against the group's known modern geography.

**MAEVRA:** If the rest of this matches, Caelora should be around there.

**TORREN:** Around the break, yeah.

He studies the missing section.

**TORREN:** Whatever stood there, it was the biggest concentration on this part of the map. Center's gone.

**CYANIS:** Of course it is.

No one attempts to draw through the break or identify the missing ancient city.

**MAEVRA:** Copy what's here.

**CYANIS:** Works for me.

Torren glances at him.

**TORREN:** See? Trainable.

Cyanis gives him a look.

**CYANIS:** Careful.

Torren goes back to the map.

## [PRACTICAL COPY]

The party makes a physical copy of as much surviving information as practical.

A brief fade/state transition is enough.

The copy preserves:
- the broad western ancient-Diyse geography;
- the dense overland route network;
- the distinct second system of markings, still unidentified by the party;
- the numerous surviving city / settlement clusters;
- the largest concentration around the geography of present-day Caelora and the exact shape/location of the physical break through its center;
- the southeast alignment corresponding to the party's existing route toward Dunmere;
- the six Face markings.

The party does **not** reconstruct or invent the missing Caelora-area center.

Dunmere is not discovered here. It was already the party's next practical destination.

## [CAMP DECISION]

Once the useful information is copied, Maevra looks toward the southern road.

**MAEVRA:** Dunmere tonight?

Torren answers without hesitation.

**TORREN:** No. Road's easy enough in daylight and annoying in the dark. After today, there's no reason to make it worse.

**MAEVRA:** All right.

**TORREN:** Camp here. Dunmere in the morning.

Cyanis looks around the quiet Junction.

**CYANIS:** Works for me.

Ilyra exhales.

**ILYRA:** Good. I was prepared to be a pain in the ass.

Maevra looks at her.

**MAEVRA:** Prepared?

**ILYRA:** More of one.

**MAEVRA:** There she is.

Torren looks toward the campable ground nearby.

**TORREN:** I'll find us a decent spot.

**CYANIS:** One without any more ancient bullshit?

Torren looks back at him.

**TORREN:** That's the goal.
