# Diyse — Knowledge Firewall Invisibility Rule

**Status:** CURRENT EXPLICIT USER DIRECTION  
**Domain:** dialogue authoring / character knowledge presentation  
**Purpose:** keep reveal discipline in the authoring system without making characters repeatedly explain their own uncertainty.

## Hard rule

> **The knowledge firewall belongs primarily in the writer, scene spec, and Canon Checker — not in the characters' mouths.**

Characters should know only what they can plausibly know, but they should not repeatedly announce that boundary with lines such as:
- "that's all I know";
- "that's all I can tell from here";
- "maybe" after every observation;
- "that's an observation, not an answer";
- repeated self-corrections whose only purpose is to demonstrate canon compliance;
- other dialogue that sounds like the character is performing the writer's reveal firewall.

## Preferred behavior

Use ordinary human speech.

- Let a character state the useful thing they actually know and stop talking.
- Let silence preserve uncertainty.
- Let the environment leave gaps the party does not fill.
- Allow speculation when it is natural; characters may guess, disagree, be wrong, or leave a theory hanging.
- Use "I don't know," "maybe," or a qualifier only when the conversational moment genuinely calls for it, not as a repeated signature proving the character is careful.
- Expertise should appear through useful decisions, attention, and action — not through constant disclaimers.

## Magic / Card / Prime application

The lived-magic firewall must also remain invisible in ordinary speech.

For ordinary Abilities and Standard Cards:
- characters simply treat them as normal parts of life;
- do not make characters explain that magic is normal;
- do not make characters explain the basic function of Standard Cards to informed adults;
- do not add artificial awe merely to prove the setting contains magic.

For Prime Cards:
- characters do not need to append "if Primes are real," "according to the records," or "we don't know what they do" every time the term appears;
- use those distinctions when the actual conversation/evidence requires them;
- otherwise let uncertainty live in what the character does **not** claim;
- a Card specialist may distinguish record, rumor, inference, and fact naturally, but should not sound like a walking canon disclaimer.

The invisible Canon Checker owns the hard firewall:
- no verified modern Prime holder/use before story evidence;
- no operational Prime mechanics before discovery;
- no author-only Story-Prime association leaking into character knowledge.

## Torren-specific application

Torren remains evidence-first and comfortable with uncertainty, but this must not become a verbal tic.

Prefer:
- a route choice;
- pointing out one concrete sign;
- changing course without narrating every inference;
- answering a direct question plainly;
- saying nothing when he has nothing useful to add.

Do not make Cyanis or other characters repeatedly joke about Torren's qualifiers. The occasional joke can land once; repeated "there's the qualifier" exchanges turn a character trait into authorial commentary.

## Maevra-specific application

Maevra may stop dangerous overconfidence or unsupported operational assumptions when stakes require it. She should not act as the scene's permanent Canon Checker by repeatedly saying variations of "we don't know" or "don't turn that into an answer."

## Investigation / mystery scenes

Mystery dialogue may contain competing interpretations. It does not need to resolve every line into a formally bounded statement.

Bad rhythm:
> observation → guess → correction → disclaimer → restatement of what is known

Preferred rhythm:
> observation → reaction or competing guess → silence / next action

The Canon Checker verifies afterward that no unsupported guess was accidentally presented as settled fact.

## Editing test

Before keeping a qualifier/disclaimer, ask:

> **Would this person naturally say this here if the writer were not worried about spoiling future canon?**

If no, remove it and preserve the knowledge boundary invisibly.
