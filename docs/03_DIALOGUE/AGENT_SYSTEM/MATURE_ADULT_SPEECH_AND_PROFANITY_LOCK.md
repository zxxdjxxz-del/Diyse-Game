# Mature Adult Speech and Profanity Lock

**Status:** ACTIVE DIALOGUE-ENGINE CRAFT AUTHORITY  
**Domain:** spoken character performance across all current and future dialogue passes  
**Applies to:** Person-Agent rehearsal, Dialogue Editor passes, character-balance audits, Character-Life scenes, mainline story dialogue, walking dialogue where otherwise legal, and later chapter generation.

## Core rule

> **DIYSE characters are mature adults speaking like mature adults. Do not sanitize them into polite JRPG dialogue, and do not force profanity into mouths where it does not belong.**

Natural adult speech may include:
- profanity;
- vulgarity;
- insults;
- fragments;
- interruptions;
- talking over one another;
- unfinished thoughts;
- self-correction;
- blunt disagreement;
- sexual or bodily language where ordinary adult context supports it;
- crude jokes;
- affectionate insults;
- saying the socially imperfect thing and then revising it.

None of these are mandatory in every scene. They are available because the cast are adults, not because the script is trying to appear edgy.

## Profanity is character behavior, not tonal garnish

Profanity must come from:
- the speaker's established habits;
- relationship safety/familiarity;
- anger, fear, pain, fatigue, surprise, frustration, delight, or amusement;
- professional culture;
- the social register of the room;
- the rhythm of the sentence itself.

Do **not**:
- insert swearing at fixed intervals;
- make every character swear at the same frequency;
- give every character the same swear vocabulary;
- censor a character who would naturally swear merely because the scene is serious;
- make profanity disappear simply because an authority figure is present;
- use constant profanity as a substitute for personality, maturity, danger, or intimacy.

A clean line can be more truthful than a profane one. A profane line can be more truthful than a clean one. The character and moment decide.

## Current character calibration

These are qualitative performance guides, **not numeric quotas**.

### Nimera
**High / frequent / deeply integrated.**

Profanity is a major part of Nimera's natural adult speech and should survive editing.

She may swear:
- inside technical explanations;
- while correcting her own reasoning;
- because a category is stupid;
- because evidence is inconvenient;
- while joking;
- while arguing over something trivial;
- when delighted, annoyed, tired, or fascinated.

Her profanity often belongs **inside the syntax of the thought**, not bolted onto the end as a toughness marker.

Do not sanitize her because she is young, scholarly, precise, or explaining lore. She should generally be the most naturally and frequently profane permanent party member unless a specific later character authority clearly overrides that in a specific context.

True fury may make her cleaner and more formal. Genuine vulnerability may simplify her speech. Those contrasts are meaningful because ordinary Nimera is so freely profane.

### Torren
**Casual / unselfconscious / relationship-responsive.**

Torren is comfortable swearing and does not treat profanity as an event.

It may appear in:
- veteran shorthand;
- irritation;
- affectionate insult;
- practical complaint;
- stupid arguments;
- banter with Cyanis;
- later earned banter with Nimera, Vaelira, Seyrik, or others;
- ordinary conversation when the word is simply the natural one.

His increasing social comfort from Chapter 2 onward may make profanity and affectionate insult more available, but he does not need to become louder or more vulgar to prove he has opened up.

### Ilyra
**Moderate / dry / occasionally sharper than expected.**

Ilyra may swear naturally when:
- irritated;
- amused;
- tired;
- something is absurd;
- somebody has done something stupid;
- an ordinary adult conversation turns crude;
- a precise stronger word is funnier than a polite one.

Her profanity should help prevent healer/Grace coding from turning her into a saintly or sanitized woman. She can be composed and still say `fuck`.

### Cyanis
**Moderate / freer with trust and pressure.**

Cyanis is comfortable with profanity, especially:
- among people he trusts;
- during sharp affectionate banter;
- when startled or frustrated;
- after a long day when command polish has dropped;
- when a deliberately blunt word is funnier or more honest.

Formal responsibility can make him more controlled, but it does not make him prudish. He should not sound like a sanitized protagonist while everybody around him talks like adults.

### Maevra
**Moderate / register-sensitive / never sanitized by rank.**

Maevra is a senior commander and socially sophisticated. She changes register deliberately.

- formal command may be cleaner, shorter, and more controlled;
- field stress can produce blunt profanity;
- with Cyanis and Ilyra, whom she already knows very well, she can swear naturally without erasing the command hierarchy;
- with Torren, old familiarity makes profanity and affectionate insult especially available;
- off duty she may be quicker, more profane, competitive, gossipy, and vulgar when the context supports it.

Rank changes the social meaning of the line. It does **not** require sanitization.

### Other current/future characters
Use the owning character authority to establish their individual register. Until a stronger habit is explicitly established, default to mature adult naturalism rather than either total cleanliness or automatic profanity.

## Rank, professionalism, and familiarity

Professional adults do not all speak formally all the time.

A character may know another person extremely well and still outrank them.

For relationships such as **Maevra with Cyanis and Ilyra**:
- established personal familiarity permits shorthand, teasing, bluntness, and natural profanity;
- operational context still preserves Maevra's senior authority;
- Cyanis/Ilyra do not become insolent peers merely because everyone is comfortable;
- Maevra does not become distant or sanitized merely because she is issuing an order.

Register should shift with context rather than flatten the relationship in either direction.

## Mature-adult naturalism audit

Every dedicated character or ensemble dialogue pass must now check:

1. **Does the character sound like an adult rather than a sanitized archetype?**
2. **Is profanity available at the frequency and style that belongs to this specific character?**
3. **Has the editor accidentally cleaned away profanity that was part of the Person Brain's natural rhythm?**
4. **Has profanity been added where it feels performative, repetitive, juvenile, or out of character?**
5. **Do different characters have distinct registers rather than one shared "mature" voice?**
6. **Does rank/formality alter register without erasing established familiarity?**
7. **Do trusted relationships gain freer language gradually where story progression has earned it?**
8. **Can serious scenes contain profanity without automatically turning comic, and can comic scenes stay funny without requiring profanity?**

A pass is **not complete** merely because plot facts and character roles are correct if the spoken language still sounds artificially cleaned, uniformly polite, or written for an audience younger than the cast.

## No quota rule

There is no target number of swear words per scene, chapter, or character.

Do not solve this lock by counting `fuck`, `shit`, `bitch`, or any other word.

The correct question is:
> **Would this particular adult, in this particular relationship and state, actually phrase the thought this way?**

## Editor preservation rule

The Dialogue Editor may improve rhythm and remove redundant profanity, but it must not systematically convert:
- `fuck` → `damn`;
- `shit` → `mess`;
- vulgar adult jokes → generic teasing;
- affectionate insults → polite banter;
- character-specific bluntness → neutral exposition

merely to make dialogue cleaner.

Cleaning is valid only when the cleaner wording is more truthful to the speaker and scene.
