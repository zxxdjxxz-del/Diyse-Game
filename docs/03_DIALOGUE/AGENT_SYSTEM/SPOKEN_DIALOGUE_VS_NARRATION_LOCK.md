# DIYSE Dialogue — Spoken Dialogue vs Narration Lock

**Status:** CURRENT DIALOGUE PRODUCTION RULE  
**Applies to:** all current and future mainline dialogue, Character-Life scenes, character quests, side quests, and dialogue revisions  
**Origin:** Chapter-3 royal-voice / narration-reduction audit, 2026-09-12

## Purpose

DIYSE characters must sound like people acting inside the scene, not like the production document speaking through them.

A line can be factually correct, in-character on the surface, and still be wrong if its real function is to explain to the player what the scene has already established.

The default rule is:

> **The environment shows. Evidence owners interpret. Authority figures decide. Characters react. Nobody recites the scene back to the player.**

## 1. Do not verbalize visible information merely for clarity

If the player has just watched or inspected a fact, do not have a character immediately restate the same fact in complete explanatory form.

Bad pattern:
- environment shows several increasingly accurate copied seals;
- character then says, “First they copied Ancient seals, then Yahtrean seals, then royal-document seals, and finally the Crest.”

Preferred pattern:
- the environment carries the progression;
- a character reacts to the implication: “Same work kept getting cleaner.” / “And more specific.”

A spoken line should add judgment, uncertainty, personality, consequence, conflict, or action—not duplicate staging.

## 2. Authority figures convert information into decisions

Queens, commanders, ministers, officers, and other authority figures should not prove that they followed a briefing by repeating it.

For **Queen Lysara**, especially:
- ask the decisive question;
- test certainty;
- establish a boundary;
- assign responsibility;
- issue an order;
- make a judgment.

Target Lysara register:
- “Harth. Certain?”
- “Then not today.”
- “Say it plainly.”
- “No accusations without proof.”
- “Can you prove unrecorded access?”
- “Then neither is fact. Move on.”
- “Copies only.”

Avoid Lysara lines whose hidden function is: “Here is what everyone in this room and the player already knows.”

For **Mirena**, the same principle applies with a somewhat more conversational register. She converts evidence into investigative priorities, trust boundaries, and next actions rather than summarizing the clue stack.

## 3. Evidence owners may explain what only they can responsibly conclude

Narration reduction is **not** a universal brevity rule.

A scholar, medic, tracker, engineer, commander, or witness may need a longer turn when interpretation itself is their job.

Nimera may say:

> “I can prove they made the mark. I can prove they tried to make it more than a mark. I cannot prove they ever got the royal response.”

That line is valid because the scene cannot show the evidentiary boundary by itself, and Nimera is the person qualified to establish it.

The test is not “Is this line long?”

The test is “Does this character know or decide something that the player cannot obtain merely by looking at the scene?”

## 4. New-listener recaps must be compressed

Sometimes a character enters after the player already knows important information. The new character genuinely needs the information, but repeating the previous scene word-for-word makes the dialogue feel like recap narration.

When briefing a new listener:
- give only what that listener needs to respond;
- compress facts the player already knows;
- let the listener ask for the detail that matters to them;
- do not recreate the prior scene chronologically.

Preferred rhythm:
1. consequence first;
2. listener asks the useful question;
3. evidence owner supplies the requested detail;
4. scene moves forward.

Do not use a new listener as an excuse to replay the previous exposition.

## 5. Reports may be complete without becoming recaps

Formal reports are allowed to contain information. The report owner should normally finish one coherent report unit before interruption.

However:
- report the result, not every beat the player experienced;
- emphasize information the receiving authority needs;
- questions should test provenance, consequence, uncertainty, or required action;
- do not make every present character take a turn summarizing their specialty.

A report scene is still a scene between people, not a chapter summary.

## 6. Repetition requires a new dramatic function

A fact may be repeated only when the repetition does something new, such as:
- challenges its reliability;
- changes who now knows it;
- confirms exact wording;
- changes a decision;
- exposes a contradiction;
- becomes a joke or emotional beat rooted in character;
- establishes that a speaker misunderstood it.

Simple repetition for player clarity is not enough.

## 7. Questions should not exist only to prompt exposition

Avoid mechanical prompt-answer chains:
- “What happened?”
- exposition;
- “And then?”
- exposition;
- “What does that mean?”
- exposition.

Questions should arise from what the speaker cares about and should narrow the problem.

Examples:
- “Certain?”
- “Did any of them answer like the Queen’s?”
- “How far apart?”
- “Exact wording?”
- “Can you prove unrecorded access?”

## 8. Character voice outranks explanatory completeness

Do not make a character unnaturally precise because the production document wants a fact stated.

Torren may give bounded physical observations.  
Ilyra may identify practical risk or firsthand condition.  
Nimera may establish scholarly distinctions.  
Cyanis may notice, challenge, joke, or act without becoming the audience surrogate for every explanation.  
Maevra may report command-relevant facts without becoming a co-narrator.  
Lysara and Mirena should exercise authority rather than summarize.

Selective participation remains valid. A character does not need a line merely because they are present.

## 9. Environment-first test

Before keeping an explanatory line, ask:

1. Did the player just see this?
2. Did another character just say this?
3. Is this line only translating production intent into speech?
4. Would the scene still be understandable if the line were removed?
5. If the information must be spoken, who actually owns it and what is the shortest natural form that preserves their character?

If the first three answers are yes, the line is presumptively narration disguised as dialogue.

## 10. Dialogue-editor pass requirement

Every production dialogue pass should include a dedicated **spoken-vs-narration audit** after canon/knowledge checks and before final synchronization.

Flag:
- evidence recaps;
- visible-action recaps;
- authority figures repeating reports;
- new-listener exposition dumps;
- dialogue that verbalizes reveal firewalls;
- characters explaining the thematic meaning of their own behavior;
- conclusion repetition by multiple speakers;
- lines that sound like production notes with a character name attached.

A flagged line should be removed, compressed, reassigned to the true evidence owner, or converted into a question/judgment/action.

## Lock

This rule governs future DIYSE dialogue unless the user explicitly revises it.

> **Characters live the scene. The production document explains the scene. Never make the characters do the production document’s job.**
