# DIYSE — Whole-Game Scene Authoring Standard

**Status:** ACTIVE / REQUIRED  
**Effective:** August 18, 2026  
**Scope:** All new story, Character-Life, hub, quest, Hunt-linked, battle-linked, interlude, and aftermath scene authoring from Chapter 4 forward, plus any later explicitly reopened scene.

This standard is a permanent project workflow guardrail. It applies to Chapters 4–12, the Sixfold Accord, optional scenes, Hunts where dialogue/staging is authored, and `WORLD_AFTER` content unless a later explicit user correction supersedes it.

It does **not** reopen Chapters 0–3.

## Core rule

Before writing each scene, briefly check the scene against the current canon and the characters who are actually participating. Then write/review the scene at a normal useful scene-sized scope. Do not split scenes into tiny blocks merely to perform these checks.

The game should continue to feel like a **2.5D JRPG with exploration and combat**, not a sequence of back-to-back conversations.

## Mandatory pre-scene check

Before drafting each scene, verify:

### 1. Story / continuity

- What must this scene accomplish?
- What happened immediately before it and what must it hand off to afterward?
- Are location, time, geography, roster state, guest/permanent-party state, equipment/Card/Prime state, and chapter progression correct?
- Does the scene preserve all already-locked outcomes and avoid reopening closed canon accidentally?

### 2. Knowledge firewall

- What does each participant actually know **at this exact point**?
- Do not leak later truths about Ancient Diyse, the Entity, the Underground Crest Network, Story Primes, later antagonists, future relationships, or any other unrevealed material.
- Characters may infer from evidence they possess, but must not speak from player knowledge or master-canon knowledge they have not earned.

### 3. Personality / voice

- Re-check the current personality and voice anchors of every meaningful participant.
- Characters must sound like themselves rather than variants of one generic witty JRPG voice.
- Adult characters may swear, joke, interrupt, misunderstand, gossip, get petty, be tired, say very little, change subjects, or leave thoughts unfinished when natural.
- Do not make someone speak merely because they are present.
- Genuine danger changes behavior: humor can drop immediately; competence and established stress responses take over.

### 4. Relationship state

- Check the current relationship stage for the pairs who actually interact.
- Do not jump ahead in familiarity, intimacy, nicknames, trust, hostility, forgiveness, or emotional disclosure.
- Let relationships progress through behavior and accumulated moments instead of explanatory speeches.
- Preserve established pair-specific rhythms rather than forcing every new party member into every existing dynamic.

### 5. Adult dialogue + expressive JRPG/anime performance

- Dialogue should sound like real adults talking, not people announcing their emotions or explaining the scene to the audience.
- Reactions may be more visually expressive than real life: portrait swaps, sudden stares, head snaps, recoils, deadpan holds, annoyed freezes, trying not to laugh, exaggerated side-eye, posture changes, small sprite turns/movements, camera timing, and silence.
- Let the **performance** carry information that does not need to be spoken.
- Use silence as participation.
- Comedy should be timed through pauses/reactions, not only punchlines.

### 6. Affordable 2.5D production

Every authored scene must remain practical for Diyse's affordable 2.5D presentation.

Prefer:

- reusable stand/sit/walk/interaction/casting poses;
- portrait/expression swaps;
- small sprite shifts/turns;
- camera framing, pans, inserts, and holds;
- ordinary reusable props such as maps, papers, cups, food, tools, Cards, bandages, reports, chairs, tables, weapons, and field gear;
- lighting/VFX changes;
- authored environment before/after states;
- layered background workers/crowds rather than crowd AI;
- prepared gate, water-level, machinery, route, damage, and room states rather than simulation.

Avoid requiring expensive bespoke animation, physics destruction, fluid simulation, complex crowd behavior, or one-off cinematic choreography when the same meaning can be delivered with the established 2.5D grammar.

### 7. Gameplay breathing room

Do not stack mandatory dialogue scenes continuously when the chapter fiction supports play between them.

Use the rhythm established in the completed chapters:

**dialogue/story beat → traversal/exploration → combat-capable space where appropriate → environmental discovery/interaction → next authored dialogue beat**

The exact rhythm may vary by scene, but the player should regularly regain control and have room to move through the world.

### 8. Random battles

Traditional random encounters remain Diyse's ordinary hostile-exploration layer where the fiction supports them.

- Build **real traversal space between dialogue beats** when the route/location is hostile or unsecured.
- Random-battle areas should be long enough to breathe and support exploration/combat, but should not become huge empty zones merely to add encounter time.
- Use chapter-appropriate enemy variety and avoid making every encounter group identical.
- Safe settlements, secured hubs, immediate story pockets, delicate NPC sequences, and other authored safe states may suppress random encounters.
- A safe/story pocket suppresses encounter triggering locally; it does not require pretending the surrounding hostile area has become safe.
- Do not force random battles into scenes/locations where they make no fictional or pacing sense.

### 9. Authored encounters / bosses / Hunts when relevant

If the scene touches a mandatory battle, authority encounter, boss, Hunt branch, or optional-major route:

- check the controlling encounter study and exact chapter lock;
- preserve nonlethal/lethal intent;
- preserve boss HP-bar/state architecture;
- preserve first-pass denied branches and post-climax return grammar where applicable;
- do not invent extra mandatory fights simply because the scene feels quiet;
- keep random battles distinct from authored mandatory encounters.

### 10. Scene handoff

Before calling a scene draft ready for user review, check:

- what state the player exits the scene in;
- whether control returns to exploration, combat-capable traversal, a safe hub, another mandatory scene, or an optional window;
- whether the next scene's trigger and character availability make sense;
- whether the chapter has enough space between major conversations.

## Review / acceptance workflow

- Draft and review **scene by scene** at normal useful size.
- The user may correct individual lines, staging, logic, performance, gameplay spacing, or continuity without forcing a full rewrite.
- Explicit user corrections control immediately.
- Once a scene or defined scene scope is accepted, preserve its exact accepted wording/staging in the chapter source and record the acceptance.
- Later corrections supersede earlier accepted material explicitly; do not silently erase change history.
- Complete the chapter's mandatory scenes **and** its associated optional Character-Life/hub scenes before calling the chapter fully finished.
- After the chapter is accepted, run the whole-chapter pass for continuity, character voice, relationship progression, repetition, gameplay pacing, random-battle spacing, enemy variety, Hunts/optional content, knowledge firewall, 2.5D affordability, and chapter handoff before production Resource conversion.

## Standing intent

This is the default authoring method for the rest of DIYSE. Future chapter work should begin from this standard automatically rather than re-deriving the workflow each time.
