## Beat purpose
Beat 11 is the chapter's first major confrontation with an Ancient guardian tied to the Old City's deeper civic / command chambers.

It should:
1. pay off Beat 10's trail into the oldest protected rooms beyond the Hall of Seals;
2. move the party from manuscript research and historical seal collections into a place where Ancient authority was physically exercised;
3. present the **First Command Warden** as an old magical guardian bound to that chamber, not as a speaking villain or computer-like security system;
4. give the completed four-person party a major story boss encounter;
5. preserve the Warden's role as a guardian of a real physical place rather than an exposition device;
6. reveal the exact phrases **PREVIOUS ERROR** and **LAST SENTINEL CONFIRMED** through physical Ancient inscription after the battle;
7. let Cyanis's Card change from its earlier unstable green-and-gold behavior into a stable **deep Ruby** state only after that revelation;
8. make the relationship between the inscription and the Card undeniable enough to matter but still insufficient to identify the Card conclusively;
9. keep Prime / Might / Last Sentinel classification unresolved until the later evidence-consolidation beat;
10. open the protected way into Beat 12's Ancient seal-working chamber and northern-route evidence.

Beat 11 is:
> **protected chamber → guardian confrontation → cryptic Ancient inscription → stable Ruby Card response.**

It is not a computer-authentication scene.

---

- the permanent active party is **Cyanis + Ilyra + Torren + Nimera**;

- Cyanis's Card remains physically with him;

- no one has established Cyanis as its bearer / owner in a formal magical sense;
- the Card has previously shown unstable green-and-gold light but has not yet reached its later stable deep Ruby state.

## Location identity — the protected civic chambers
The architecture beyond the Hall of Seals should feel like the party has crossed out of a library and into **the preserved heart of an old civic institution**.

Favor physical fantasy language:
- broad fitted stone corridors;
- dark metal braces / bands set directly into the masonry;
- carved seal emblems over thresholds;
- recessed ward-lines filled with old mineral / metal inlay;
- heavy doors or shutters that physically withdraw into the walls;
- worn reliefs of officials / sentinels / seal-bearers where appropriate;
- deep floor rings used for formal standing positions or ceremonies;
- old lamp niches or magical stone that still holds weak light;
- sealed side rooms with physical bars, slabs, or interlocking stone-metal closures;
- ceremonial stone tables / plinths / seal-rests;
- signs that these chambers once handled decisions, orders, sealed instructions, or protected civic functions.

Do not describe:
- interfaces;
- permissions;
- credentials;
- login-like recognition;
- scanning;
- data;
- network states;
- access protocols.

The party is walking through **an ancient magical command complex**, not a server room.

That uncertainty is useful.

## The Warden chamber
The boss chamber should be visually distinct and memorable.

Recommended presentation:
- a large circular or long ceremonial hall;
- one massive sealed doorway / stone-metal shutter beyond the chamber;
- carved floor rings or seal motifs converging around the central space;
- worn reliefs / inscriptions around the walls;
- one dormant guardian form integrated into a wall recess, plinth, or standing position;
- old metal and stone fitted together with clear magical craft;
- very little modern Yahtrean intervention.

The room should feel like it once had a serious public / civic function, not merely like a boss arena.

## The First Command Warden
The Warden is an **Ancient guardian construct** tied to the protected chamber.

It can read as:
- a large armored stone-and-metal sentinel;
- a ceremonial guardian statue that becomes animate;
- a heavy humanoid construct with seal plates worked into its armor;
- a guardian built partly into the chamber that tears free / steps down when the ward is disturbed.

### Visual tone
Favor:
- engraved plates;
- warded joints;
- old seal marks;
- physical weaponry or magical ward arms;
- heavy, deliberate movement;
- old craft that feels ceremonial and military at once.

Avoid:
- floating holographic panels;
- laser scanners;
- robotic voice chatter;
- UI-like targeting projections;
- digital glitches;
- screen readouts.

### No speaking villain
The Warden does not need dialogue.

Its intent is legible through action:
- the chamber is protected;
- the party has crossed a threshold;
- the Warden wakes and places itself between them and the sealed inner way;
- when they do not / cannot satisfy whatever old condition once allowed peaceful passage, it attacks.

The party does not need to know the exact original rule.

---

## Why the Warden attacks
The reason should remain bounded.

What the party can observe:
- they entered a protected Ancient civic chamber;
- the Warden awakened only after they crossed / disturbed the inner threshold;
- it deliberately blocks the protected doorway beyond;
- it reacts to their presence as unacceptable under whatever ward / oath / command condition governs the chamber.

What they cannot yet prove:
- whether the Warden expected a specific office-holder;
- whether it was testing a seal;
- whether it was reacting to Cyanis's Card;
- whether it recognized any party member;
- whether the recent reader triggered it earlier;
- whether its original criteria still function exactly as intended.

Do not write the Warden as if it has run an identity check.

It is enough that an old guardian ward rejects passage.

## The Ancient inscription — physical, not digital
After the Warden settles, a physical change in the chamber reveals two short Ancient phrases.

Possible fantasy presentation:
- old lettering previously hidden beneath tarnish or darkness fills with faint light;
- engraved characters around the Warden's plinth become legible;
- metal-inlaid letters brighten one after another along a stone band;
- a warded inscription previously obscured by the Warden's active seal becomes visible when the guardian goes dormant.

Nimera / existing scholarship can provide the usable modern translation.

The required phrases are:
> **PREVIOUS ERROR**
>
> **LAST SENTINEL CONFIRMED**

### Presentation rule
Do **not** show these as:
- a screen;
- floating text box in-world;
- terminal message;
- error code;
- system log;
- robotic spoken announcement.

They are **Ancient words physically revealed in the room**.

The current English wording is the story translation of those words.

- a Card designation;
- a guardian rank;

- one of several possible Ancient terms.

This uncertainty is important.

## Cyanis's Card — stable deep Ruby response
Only **after** the Last Sentinel inscription becomes visible does Cyanis's Card change again.

The earlier unstable green-and-gold response:
- contracts;
- folds inward;
- reorganizes through the Card's geometry;
- then resolves into a **stable deep Ruby state**.

The exact visual language can be refined later, but the change should be unmistakable.

### What is different this time
Unlike the earlier green-and-gold light:
- the Ruby state remains stable after the immediate magical reaction ends;
- its geometry appears more settled / coherent;
- it is the clearest persistent state change the Card has shown so far.

### What does NOT happen
No:
- figure;
- voice;
- silhouette;
- summon;
- autonomous attack;
- Prime manifestation;
- Prime recovery;
- Prime use;
- new combat ability from the Card.

This is a **state change**, not a recovered Prime.
