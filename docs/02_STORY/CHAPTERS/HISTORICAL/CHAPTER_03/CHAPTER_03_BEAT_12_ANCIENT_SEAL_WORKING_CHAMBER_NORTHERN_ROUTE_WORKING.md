## Beat purpose
Beat 12 is the payoff to the chapter's Old City seal investigation.

It should:
1. begin immediately after the First Command Warden shuts down and Cyanis's Card settles into a stable deep Ruby state;
2. move the party through the newly opened protected inner way into a preserved Ancient sealwright / authority chamber;
3. reveal a **physical Diysean authority seal** and the surviving craft used to place enduring magical authority into such an object;
4. establish that magical authority could be **imbued into the seal itself** rather than requiring the authorized person to consciously cast through it every single time;
5. give the party its first concrete Ancient comparison for Queen Lysara's impossible seal behavior;
6. strongly imply that Yahtrea's magical royal-seal tradition descends from, preserves, or independently inherited much older Diysean practice;
7. keep the exact lineage of Lysara's current seal unresolved;
8. keep the exact unauthorized-use mechanism unresolved;
9. preserve the unknown recent reader mystery;
10. reveal a large physical route relief / map showing an important **unlabeled site** and a route continuing **north beyond it**;
11. let Torren copy the usable geography for comparison in Caelora;
12. end the Old City field investigation cleanly and hand into Beat 13 — Four Answers, Not One.

Beat 12 is:
> **sealwright chamber → physical magical proof → Queen-seal implications → ancient route relief → return with evidence.**

It is not another boss beat and it is not a computer-room reveal.

## Location identity — the ancient sealwright chamber
The room beyond the Warden should be substantially smaller and more purposeful than the boss chamber.

It should feel like a place where skilled people once **made, renewed, tested, or prepared authority seals**.

Favor physical fantasy details:
- heavy stone worktables worn smooth by repeated use;
- fitted metal dies / matrices set into stone housings;
- circular engraving frames sized for seals or signets;
- narrow channels of precious metal / mineral inlay running between work positions;
- shallow basins or fitted recesses for oils, powders, inks, resins, or magical compounds;
- sealed drawers / stone caskets for blanks, tools, or finished authority pieces;
- old clamps / presses / hand-operated frames strengthened by magical warding;
- etched rings where a seal could be set during magical work;
- surviving residue / discoloration where repeated enchantment took place;
- worn sigils around work stations rather than glowing abstract interfaces;
- one especially protected finished **authority seal** preserved on a central rest / cradle / stone plinth.

The room should communicate **craft**, not automation.

The Diyseans were highly advanced in magical workmanship, but the place still looks like somewhere a trained sealwright physically worked.

## The preserved Ancient authority seal
The central object should be unmistakably a serious working seal rather than decoration.

Possible physical characteristics:
- a heavy signet / stamp / seal head mounted to a handle or fitted holder;
- engraved civic / command marks;
- metal or stone unlike ordinary modern court ornamentation;
- visible wear from real use;
- fine magical inlay running through the seal body rather than merely painted on its face;
- a fitted resting cradle clearly designed to hold it between uses.

### What the party may conclude immediately
The object was:
- physically used;
- repeatedly maintained;
- magically important;
- part of the same broad family of practices as the authority seals described in the Hall of Seals.

They cannot yet conclude:
- it belonged to an emperor / monarch specifically;
- it is a direct ancestor of Lysara's exact physical seal;
- its exact office-holder or title;
- how many similar seals existed.

---

## The sealwright craft — what the room proves
The chamber must provide more than another old seal.

The surviving work arrangement should let Nimera, Cyanis, and the others establish with reasonable confidence that this room was used to **place magical authority into a physical seal**.

Evidence can include:
- concentric carved workings around a seal-rest;
- residue showing magical force repeatedly entered the seal body through those rings;
- paired work positions suggesting one source / one receiving seal;
- old tool marks showing the seal was physically seated, locked, or aligned during the process;
- inscriptions / sigils about binding, holding, carrying, oath, office, charge, mark, or equivalent concepts that survive well enough to interpret cautiously;
- comparison between the preserved seal's internal inlay and the chamber's matching engraved work rings;
- signs that the craft could also be used for later renewal / maintenance after initial creation.

### Mandatory conclusion
The party can now reasonably establish:
> **Diysean authority magic could be bound into a physical seal so the seal itself carried an enduring magical authority response.**

This explains why a legitimate royal seal can still authenticate something without the ruler consciously casting through it at that moment.

### Important boundary
The room demonstrates **imbuing / binding authority into a seal**.

It does **not** demonstrate:
- copying authority from one active seal into another;
- stealing authority from a living ruler;
- making a duplicate answer identically;
- remote invocation;
- imprinting the Queen's authority onto paper without access to her seal;
- recreating Lysara's current seal;
- transferring authority between unrelated people.

Do not let the party accidentally solve the modern crime here.

## Connection to Queen Lysara's seal
This is the most important story implication of the room.

The party now has physical evidence that:
- magical authority seals existed long before modern Yahtrea;
- the magic could reside in the physical object;
- specialized sealwright craft was involved in creating / maintaining that condition;
- the Hall of Seals' old Yahtrean renewal traditions were probably not ceremonial superstition;
- Yahtrea's working royal-seal tradition almost certainly preserves knowledge, inheritance, or practice much older than current court procedure alone would suggest.

### What becomes newly important for the Crown investigation
When the party returns, Mirena / Lysara should now care about:
- the true age of the Queen's current personal seal;
- whether it has ever been replaced;
- what object or materials preceded it;
- who historically maintained / renewed its magic;
- whether any old sealwright records survive outside the Old City;
- whether Crown tradition preserves steps whose original purpose has been forgotten;
- whether the current seal contains older internal work not visible from the outside.

### What remains unresolved
The party still does **not** know:
- whether someone physically reached Lysara's seal;
- whether a second physical seal exists;
- whether authority can be copied;
- whether the seal can be made to answer at a distance;
- how the suspect transport orders were created;
- who created them;
- Calder's hidden transport role;
- whether the Card and seal mysteries share one cause.

Beat 12 sharpens the investigation. It does not solve the culprit.

## Do not reactivate the full sealwright process
The group should not attempt to create a new authority seal.

They do not need to:
- insert Cyanis's Card into the chamber;
- use Lysara's comparison material as a magical ingredient;
- place blood / bodily material into the mechanism;
- sacrifice an existing seal;
- perform a complete ancient rite;
- recreate the authority-binding process.

The chamber's surviving physical arrangement, inscriptions, residue, matching seal construction, and Nimera's examination are enough to establish its function.

This prevents the party from becoming competent sealwrights in one scene and avoids prematurely creating a tool that could solve the modern mystery.

## The ancient route relief
A second major discovery should exist in the same protected chamber or an adjoining records alcove: a **large physical geographic relief** associated with the old civic / command complex.

Presentation should be physical and legible as ancient cartography:
- carved stone terrain;
- raised ridgelines / mountains;
- recessed river / water channels;
- fitted metal route lines;
- engraved waypoints / destination marks;
- surviving inlay highlighting major roads / protected corridors;
- worn labels, many damaged or unreadable;
- some sections broken away or lost entirely.

It may be wall-mounted, table-sized, or built into a broad stone slab.

Do not present it as a digital map or projection.

- the route geometry around it is preserved clearly enough for Torren to copy;
