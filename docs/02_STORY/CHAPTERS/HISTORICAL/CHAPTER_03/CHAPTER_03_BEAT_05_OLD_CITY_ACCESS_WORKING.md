# Chapter 3 — Beat 5 — Old City Access / Archive Descent

**Status:** CURRENT WORKING BEAT PACKET — DEVELOPED  
**Parent working structure:** `CHAPTER_03_BEAT_DEVELOPMENT_WORKING.md`  
**Primary chapter authority:** `CHAPTER_03.md`

## Beat purpose
Beat 5 moves Chapter 3 out of the palace investigation and into the **physical Old City beneath modern Caelora**.

It should:
1. give the party lawful Crown access to the Old City archives after the royal-seal review;
2. establish the Old City as a layered place where functioning modern Crown archives occupy only accessible portions of much older Diysean infrastructure;
3. pay off the Chapter-2 western-mural scale by showing that the surviving buried complex is substantially larger than an ordinary basement/archive;
4. make the transition from modern administration to Ancient architecture gradual and believable;
5. show that Crown personnel know how to operate and maintain only the parts they have actually adapted;
6. make the party actively search for **Nimera Pellan**, the expert Mirena sent them to find;
7. end immediately before Nimera's proper introduction, when the group reaches the deeper work section where she is located.

Beat 5 is **descent and spatial revelation**, not yet Nimera's character-introduction beat and not yet a combat beat.

## Starting state
- Beat 4 has confirmed that Queen Lysara's actual personal seal is physically present, intact, and functioning normally for her;
- the suspect Card orders nevertheless carry the genuine magical response associated with that seal;
- no simple recorded theft-and-return window was found;
- two broad possibilities remain open: unauthorized physical access or some unknown nonstandard way of making the seal's magic answer;
- the Queen's physical seal remains secured in the palace and does **not** travel with the party;
- Mirena has identified **Nimera Pellan** as one of Caelora's strongest Card experts and as someone likely to be useful when confronted with an impossible magical-response problem;
- Mirena has arranged lawful access to the relevant Old City archive areas;
- Cyanis still physically carries the recovered Card by Lysara's explicit decision;
- the combat-capable party is still Cyanis + Ilyra + Torren;
- Maevra remains physically present and non-playable;
- the party carries only the seal/Card comparison evidence and descriptions they are authorized to take;
- Torren still has the western-mural copy showing the enormous Ancient city at modern Caelora's location and the separate exact modern-Crest-marked structure north of it;
- the party does not yet know the buried city's original name, full extent, function, or relationship to the modern capital.

## Transition from palace to Old City
The move from royal district to Old City access should feel like a **change in civic layer**, not a teleport into a dungeon.

Possible implementation rhythm:
- short authored transition through Caelora or a controlled player walk;
- arrival at a Crown archive / records complex built into older foundations;
- formal confirmation of Mirena's access authorization;
- archive staff identify where Nimera is currently working;
- player regains control inside the modern archive layer.

The party is not sneaking in.

They are entering under valid authority to consult a specific expert and permitted records.

### No containment residue
Do not revive any retired implication that:
- the party requires movement permission because it is detained;
- guards are escorting them as suspects;
- the Card is evidence being transported under armed custody;
- lawful Crown personnel are hostile obstacles.

This is ordinary restricted-access procedure responding to an extraordinary investigation.

## Upper Old City archive — modern Crown layer
The first archive spaces should read clearly as **modern Yahtrean/Caeloran use**.

Useful environmental language:
- shelving and paper bundles;
- labelled storage cases;
- sealed record boxes;
- clerks / archivists working at tables;
- cataloguing stations;
- lamps and portable work lights;
- ladders / rolling steps / platforms where appropriate;
- modern partitions or timber/metal fittings added inside much older rooms;
- Crown locks and posted restricted-area markings on doors the Crown actively uses;
- ordinary carts / trays for moving documents;
- climate / moisture protection where practical;
- guards only where sensitive archives reasonably require them.

The space should feel used, maintained, and bureaucratically alive.

### Important contrast
The Crown layer should not disguise the older structure completely.

Even in the most adapted rooms, the party can see that shelves and partitions sit **inside something older and larger**:
- fitted stone-metal wall sections;
- oversized seams / structural joints that do not match modern masonry;
- recessed tracks no longer in ordinary use;
- sealed apertures larger than modern doors;
- floor channels / plates incorporated into later walking surfaces;
- massive support geometry that predates the archive furniture around it.

The player should understand that modern Caelora did not simply build an archive in an ordinary cellar.

It occupies surviving portions of a much older installation.

## What the Crown actually controls
The modern Crown should have **real but bounded competence** here.

Archive personnel know:
- which currently used passages are safe;
- which modern doors / locks / barriers they control;
- where mapped storage rooms and workspaces are;
- where Nimera has been assigned / working;
- which deeper sections are normally closed, unstable, inaccessible, or dependent on mechanisms they do not fully control;
- what local safety rules exist for staff entering older sections.

They do **not** know:
- the complete original layout of the buried city;
- the purpose of every Ancient mechanism;
- the full route network beneath modern Caelora;
- the original authority hierarchy of the site;
- why some Ancient systems still respond;
- the relationship between modern Crests and Diysean systems;
- the identity of Cyanis's Card;
- the solution to the Queen-seal anomaly.

This keeps Crown competence believable without making the Old City already solved by generations of archivists.

## Why the deeper areas remain only partly understood
The Old City has been present beneath Caelora for a long time, so the game needs a credible reason why unexplored / uncontrolled portions still exist.

The answer should be cumulative rather than a single magical excuse:
- many passages are physically sealed by Ancient barriers rather than modern locks;
- some old lifts / tracks / doors respond inconsistently or not at all;
- sections have been structurally unsafe, inaccessible, or not worth risking personnel for routine archive work;
- Crown archivists prioritize recoverable records and useful stable rooms over forcing every unknown mechanism;
- responsive systems can change access conditions unexpectedly;
- safe routes are mapped, while deeper sections remain partial / provisional.

The Crown therefore **uses the Old City without mastering it**.

This is important for the whole chapter.

## Nimera is the destination
The group already knows exactly who they are looking for.

Archive staff should identify:
- **Nimera Pellan** by name;
- that she is currently working deeper inside the restricted archive layers;
- the general work section / route she took;
- that she had legitimate reason to be there;
- that she is known for spending long stretches buried in difficult material / mechanisms when she thinks something matters.

Do not frame her as missing in a melodramatic sense unless later dialogue naturally uses mild irritation that she has not checked back in.

The party is going to **find the expert Mirena sent them to**, not solve a rescue mystery.

## First movement through the archive
Once normal control returns, Beat 5 should provide a meaningful but contained exploration stretch.

Suggested structure:

### Section A — adapted stacks
- mostly modern archive presentation;
- no random hostile Ancient machinery yet;
- optional environmental examinations can establish what kinds of records the Crown stores here;
- NPC archivists may provide narrow local information;
- navigation remains clear and controlled.

### Section B — transition corridors
The Crown modifications thin out.

Visible changes:
- less shelving / fewer modern walls;
- older surfaces dominate;
- ceiling / corridor scale becomes more obviously Ancient;
- inactive mechanical tracks / recessed rings / plates become more common;
- modern lamps may be mounted onto surfaces that were clearly never designed for them;
- Crown safety markings identify known dead ends or restricted side passages.

### Section C — deeper work route
The party follows the authorized route used by Nimera.

Here:
- modern foot traffic is sparse;
- Crown equipment appears temporary / portable rather than built in;
- surviving Ancient mechanisms are more intact;
- some doors / joints may move in ways modern staff cannot reproduce on demand;
- the area begins to resemble the infrastructure implied by the Chapter-2 mural rather than simply a historical archive.

This progression gives the player a physical sense of moving **under the modern capital and backward through layers of use**.

## Paying off the Chapter-2 mural scale
Beat 5 should not reveal the entire buried city, but it should make the mural feel increasingly credible.

Useful spatial evidence:
- a view across a large buried void / lower structural span;
- a partially exposed multi-level wall or transit shaft;
- blocked routes continuing beyond current Crown adaptation;
- old doors or tracks leading into volumes far larger than the rooms the Crown currently occupies;
- glimpses of descending infrastructure that make clear the known archive is only a fraction of what survives.

The correct reaction is:
> **There really is a much larger Ancient structure beneath Caelora than the modern archive footprint suggests.**

The party still cannot say:
- how much of the original city survives;
- whether all of it was one continuous complex;
- what happened to the rest;
- whether the mural represented the city exactly as it physically existed at one moment;
- what the Ancient city was called.

## Card behavior in Beat 5
The recovered Card should **not** suddenly solve navigation or authenticate Cyanis to the Old City in this beat.

Allowed:
- the party remains aware that Cyanis is carrying it;
- a subtle observed response may be noted only if already compatible with established pre-Chapter-3 behavior and if it does not add a new classification;
- Cyanis can be cautious around active mechanisms because of prior unexplained responses.

Not allowed:
- automatic door opening because Cyanis is the bearer;
- Ancient systems identifying him by name / role;
- Prime terminology;
- Might identification;
- Last Sentinel wording;
- a stable deep Ruby state;
- manifestation / recovery.

Those later discoveries must retain their impact.

## Maevra's function
Maevra remains with the group through Beat 5.

Her useful functions:
- carries / understands the Crown authorization under which they are entering;
- keeps the investigation tied to the actual question Mirena assigned rather than letting every interesting ruin become an immediate detour;
- respects archive safety procedure rather than treating Crown archivists as obstacles;
- can distinguish what the party is authorized to examine from what requires Nimera / deeper expertise;
- remains non-playable if any implementation hazard occurs.

She should not become the archive guide or Ancient expert.

## Torren's function
Torren should be especially useful in reading the **space**, not the lore.

He may notice:
- route branching;
- structural depth;
- places where modern construction is inserted into older corridors;
- inaccessible continuations;
- which paths show recent human use;
- practical signs of where Nimera / archive workers have passed;
- similarities in route scale / geometry to the western mural without claiming an exact match.

His evidence-first discipline remains intact.

## Cyanis's function
Cyanis:
- still carries the Card;
- recognizes when some geometric / structural forms feel reminiscent of Crest principles only at a broad observational level if needed;
- does **not** solve any mechanism in Beat 5;
- remains one of the firsthand witnesses for the Card if Nimera asks later.

The actual Crest-geometry cooperation belongs to Beat 7.

## Ilyra's function
Ilyra:
- keeps practical attention on safety as the environment shifts from staffed archive to uncertain Ancient infrastructure;
- can notice the human difference between a maintained workplace and an area where help would be slower to reach;
- does not become the historian or mechanic;
- does not treat ordinary archive staff as incapable simply because they do not understand every Ancient system.

## Archive personnel / knowledge provenance
If archive NPCs speak about the site, keep their knowledge local and credible.

They can say things such as:
- which sections Crown staff actively use;
- where Nimera went;
- which route is currently considered safe;
- that certain barriers have never responded to modern controls;
- that some old mechanisms react unpredictably;
- that deeper work requires specific authorization.

They should not reveal future story information because they happen to work near it.

No archivist casually knows:
- Prime;
- Last Sentinel;
- Might classification for Cyanis's Card;
- the truth of the royal seal tradition;
- Cresthaven's relation to the Ancient route;
- Calder's hidden role.

## No combat requirement
Beat 5 does **not** need combat.

The Old City should earn atmosphere before it becomes hostile.

The first mandatory hostile machine response is reserved for Beat 8 after Nimera is introduced and freed, allowing her combat usefulness to be revealed naturally.

Possible noncombat hazards are acceptable if light and implementation-friendly:
- inactive lift gaps;
- partially closed passages;
- narrow detours around damaged flooring;
- modern safety barriers around unstable sections.

Do not turn Beat 5 into a trap gauntlet.

## End approach — Nimera's work section
Near the end of the beat, the party reaches the deeper section archive staff identified as Nimera's current work area.

Signs should establish that they have found the **correct place** before they have fully met her:
- her research materials / portable lights / notes or equipment are visible on the accessible side where appropriate;
- a work table or active cataloguing setup indicates someone has been using the area recently;
- an Ancient barrier / security partition is now closed where the route should continue;
- modern Crown controls do not appear to have caused the closure;
- the barrier is visibly integrated into the Ancient structure rather than installed by the Crown.

The party may hear movement, a voice, objects being moved, or evidence that someone is beyond it.

Do not spend Nimera's proper personality introduction here.

That belongs to Beat 6.

## Beat ending
Beat 5 ends when the group establishes:
- this is Nimera's assigned deeper work area;
- an Ancient security barrier has unexpectedly closed around / across the section;
- Crown access mechanisms do not simply open it;
- Nimera is apparently on the other side / within the enclosed work area.

The immediate objective changes from **Find Nimera Pellan** to **Reach / speak with Nimera**.

Beat 6 begins with her actual introduction.

## Gameplay / authored-scene rhythm
Recommended Beat-5 rhythm:
1. transition from palace investigation to Old City archive entrance;
2. lawful access is verified;
3. modern archive layer establishes normal Crown use;
4. archive staff identify Nimera and her deeper work section;
5. player explores adapted stacks;
6. environment transitions into older Diysean corridors;
7. scale reveals show the known archive occupies only a fraction of the buried structure;
8. party follows signs / mapped route toward Nimera;
9. deeper modern modifications become temporary and sparse;
10. group reaches Nimera's work section;
11. Ancient barrier is found closed;
12. evidence establishes Nimera is beyond it;
13. handoff to Beat 6.

## Reveal firewall
Do **not** reveal in Beat 5:
- Prime classification;
- Might identification;
- Last Sentinel wording / identity;
- confirmed bearer status for Cyanis;
- stable deep Ruby Card state;
- any Prime manifestation / recovery;
- proof that modern Crests derive directly from Diysean systems;
- the ancient magical-seal imbuing process;
- whether the Queen's present seal is Ancient or inherited from Ancient practice;
- who used / invoked Lysara's seal;
- Calder's hidden transport role;
- Cresthaven;
- the identity of the northern Crest-marked structure;
- the full original name / purpose / layout of the Ancient city beneath Caelora;
- Nimera's permanent recruitment before she actually chooses to continue later.

## Beat-5 knowledge checkpoint
By the end:
- the party has entered the Old City archives lawfully under Crown authority;
- modern Crown archive space is established as an active workplace built into much older surviving Diysean infrastructure;
- the Crown meaningfully controls and understands its adapted archive areas but does not control / understand the whole buried complex;
- the physical scale strongly supports the Chapter-2 mural's implication that a much larger Ancient city once existed here;
- the full extent, original name, and purpose of that city remain unknown;
- archive staff have identified Nimera Pellan as the expert the party is seeking and directed them toward her deeper work area;
- Cyanis still carries the Card without gaining new bearer/classification information;
- Maevra remains present and non-playable;
- no mandatory combat has occurred;
- the group has reached Nimera's work section;
- an Ancient barrier has closed across/around that section beyond normal modern Crown control;
- the party has reason to believe Nimera is on the other side;
- Nimera's actual character introduction has **not yet happened**.

Handoff → **Beat 6 — Scholar in Redacted Stacks**.
