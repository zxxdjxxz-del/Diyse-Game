## Beat purpose
Beat 10 follows directly from Beat 9's discovery that **someone recently researched the same cluster of subjects as the party**.

It should:
1. let the party examine the still-open reading table and reconstruct the recent reader's research path without identifying that person yet;
2. confirm that the unknown reader was not simply browsing one topic, but deliberately cross-referencing:
   - inactive / dormant Cards;
   - green-and-gold Card light;
   - the **Diysean ruins in the Westways** from which Cyanis's Card was being transported;
   - old Yahtrean royal seals and older magical seal traditions;
3. make the research trail feel recent, purposeful, and unsettling without turning the archive into a crime-scene procedural;
4. lead the party into a more restricted historical collection known here as the **Hall of Seals**;
5. present the Hall of Seals as a physical archive of signets, seal impressions, seal-keeper manuals, old royal regalia records, engraved matrices, copied impressions, and older fragments—not as an interface or magical computer;
6. give the party stronger historical evidence that Yahtrea's magical seal tradition did not begin as a simple modern court custom;
7. establish that some old seals were treated as enduring magical objects whose authority could persist through the physical seal itself;
8. preserve the harder unanswered question of **how** that magic was originally placed into / renewed within a seal for Beat 12;
9. let the party discover that the recent reader also consulted the Hall of Seals and had been following the same historical chain deeper;
10. transition from scholarly collections into older, more restricted civic / command chambers protected by Ancient wards and guardians;
11. hand into Beat 11 — **First Command Warden**.

Beat 10 is:
> **recent research trail → historical seal evidence → deeper restricted chambers.**

It is not:
- a culprit reveal;
- the ancient seal-working discovery;
- the Last Sentinel confirmation;
- a computer-like access puzzle.

## Opening — the still-open table
Beat 10 begins at the reading space discovered at the end of Beat 9.

The table should be physical and ordinary enough to feel real:
- open books held by weights;
- one or more parchment slips marking pages;
- a folded regional map or copied survey;
- a seal-history volume left open beside Card material;
- an old Westways excavation / survey text;
- perhaps a lamp, waxed thread marker, blotting sheet, or note paper showing recent use.

The important point is not that the table contains a magical clue beacon.

It is that **somebody assembled these subjects together on purpose**.

### Recent-reader evidence
Torren can establish practical signs of recency without becoming a supernatural tracker:
- disturbed dust;
- a chair not fully pushed back;
- page edges recently handled;
- shelf gaps where books were pulled and not yet returned;
- a ladder / stool left near a relevant shelf;
- a lamp or work item recently used where plausible.

Nimera can establish scholarly meaning:
- several books normally live in completely different collections;
- the sequence of open pages follows citations / references from one subject into another;
- the reader was not casually reading about Cards, seals, or the Westways independently;
- the same person or research session appears to have been **connecting them**.

### Do not overclaim
The party should not yet know:
- who the reader was;
- whether they were Crown, private scholar, Calder's agent, Black Host, or someone else;
- whether they found the answer they were looking for;
- whether they were present minutes ago or simply earlier within a recent plausible window.

If exact recency is not physically supported, keep it qualitative: **recent**.

## Reconstructing the research path
The party should be able to infer the order in which the unknown reader likely moved through the material.

A useful chain:
1. dormant / inactive Card references;
2. accounts of unusual green-and-gold luminosity;
3. citations to finds recovered from / associated with the **Diysean ruins in the Westways**;
4. older works discussing seals / signets found near or compared against Diysean remains;
5. references pointing to restricted royal-seal collections kept deeper in the Old City.

This is important because it creates a believable reason to move toward the Hall of Seals.

The party is following **the same citations**, not following glowing footprints.

## Why the Hall of Seals exists
The Hall of Seals should be an old Yahtrean scholarly / royal collection built into older Diysean chambers.

Its purpose over the centuries:
- preserve obsolete royal seals and signets;
- preserve impressions from seals too important to keep on public display;
- store manuals / journals from seal keepers;
- document repairs, replacement, renewal, inheritance, and ceremonial transfer;
- preserve comparative examples of civic / military / noble marks recovered from older ruins;
- keep records of seals whose magic outlasted individual rulers or offices;
- preserve fragments too old / uncertain to fit normal modern classifications.

The Hall therefore feels like:
> **a royal antiquarian archive with a magical-craft history, housed inside something much older.**

It is not a throne room and not a machine room.

## Hall of Seals — physical presentation
The player should move through a substantial physical space.

Useful environmental language:
- stone alcoves containing old signets behind protective glass / crystal where appropriate;
- brass / silver / stone seal matrices;
- wax, clay, or magical-residue impressions preserved on framed sheets or tablets;
- engraved seal boxes;
- heavy cabinets with old keeper ledgers;
- suspended banners / labels from later Yahtrean cataloguing;
- locked cases containing damaged or retired royal regalia;
- carved copies of older seal faces too fragile to expose directly;
- wall recesses containing older Diysean seal fragments that later scholars grouped here by resemblance;
- reading desks with magnifying lenses, brushes, cloth, and preservation tools;
- old magical wards physically worked into cabinet frames or door lintels.

### Fantasy-presentation rule
Do not describe:
- data storage;
- authentication protocols;
- permission levels;
- interfaces;
- terminals;
- scans;
- credential checks;
- networked seal responses.

The magic should feel **crafted, inherited, physical, and partly forgotten**.

## What the Hall can establish about Yahtrean seals
Beat 10 should materially advance the Queen-seal investigation.

The party can find historical examples showing that older Yahtrean practice distinguished between:
1. **the visible seal impression** — the mark left in wax, clay, ink, foil, lacquer, or equivalent medium;
2. **the physical seal object** — the signet / matrix / stamp itself;
3. **the magical authority carried by that object** — a quality that certain working royal seals possessed beyond the visible mark.

### Stronger but still bounded finding
Some old keeper records can indicate that:
- a working royal seal could retain its recognized magical authority between uses;
- the ruler did not have to consciously cast a fresh spell into the seal every time a document was marked;
- seals sometimes required specialized renewal, restoration, blessing, binding, charging, or equivalent period craft after damage, succession, or long use;
- records of those practices become less complete farther back in Yahtrean history.

This matters enormously to Lysara's mystery.

It means the Queen's magical authority appearing on a document does **not necessarily require** that she consciously cast magic at the moment the order was sealed.

### Critical boundary
Beat 10 still does **not** prove:
- how the authority was originally placed into the seal;
- whether the seal can be used by anyone who physically possesses it;
- whether authority can be copied or transferred;
- whether remote invocation exists;
- whether Lysara's current physical seal is Ancient;
- how the suspect Card orders were produced.

Those questions remain alive.

## Older fragments — before Yahtrea
The Hall should also contain a smaller, less certain body of material recovered from Ancient Diysean contexts.

Possible pieces:
- damaged signets;
- stone / metal seal faces;
- impressions on fragments of old official material;
- carved authority emblems from civic or military ruins;
- copied scholarly sketches of pieces too fragile or lost;
- translated fragments describing seals used to bind / witness / authorize important acts.

The important conclusion is modest but significant:
> **the concept of a physical seal carrying magical civic or command authority existed before Yahtrea.**

This does not yet establish direct unbroken lineage.

Nimera should keep the language careful:
- similarity is strong;
- historical continuity is plausible;
- exact inheritance is not yet proven.

## The Westways connection inside the Hall
The research trail should connect the Hall of Seals back to the **Diysean ruins in the Westways**.

A useful form:
- an old Westways survey / excavation record mentions seal fragments, authority marks, or official-looking inscriptions recovered from the same ruin field or neighboring strata;
- later scholars cross-referenced those finds with older royal-seal studies because some shapes / magical residue were unusual;
- the evidence is incomplete enough that nobody previously concluded the Westways ruins directly produced modern Yahtrean seal practice.

### Important Card boundary
Do not reveal that Cyanis's Card and those Westways seal fragments definitely share a magical origin.

The significance is simply:
> **the ruins the Card came from were already interesting to scholars studying old authority seals.**

That makes the recent reader's research path much more suspicious / intentional.

The party discovers that the recent-reader trail continues into the Hall of Seals.

Signs can include:
- a keeper ledger already pulled from its cabinet;
- a retired seal-history volume open to a chapter on magical renewal;
- an old Westways comparison folio laid beside seal impressions;
- protective cloth moved aside on one display;
- a copied citation slip leading toward an older restricted annex;
- a cabinet key / archive tool returned improperly but not stolen, if appropriate;
- a reference volume open to a passage mentioning **older seal-working chambers** or lost seal-craft traditions.

### Do not leave an obvious signature
No convenient signed note should reveal the reader.

The party's discovery is:
> **whoever did this knew what to look for and moved through the same chain of evidence before them.**

## What Beat 10 changes about the Queen-seal mystery
Before Beat 10, the party knows:
- Lysara did not authorize the Card orders;
- her personal seal's genuine magical response appears on them;
- the physical seal is present and no simple theft window was found.

After Beat 10, they additionally know:
- older working royal seals could **retain magical authority in the physical seal itself**;
- historical seal keepers performed specialized maintenance / renewal work on such seals;
- comparable magical-seal ideas existed in Ancient Diysean material;
- the Westways ruins have old scholarly connections to authority-seal fragments / studies;
- an unknown recent reader had already been tracing this same web of evidence.

### What remains unanswered
- how magical authority is actually imbued into a seal;
- whether unauthorized use requires physical possession;
- whether the seal can be made to answer by another means;
- whether Lysara's present seal is Ancient, descendant, copy, or later construction;
- who made the Card transport orders;
- who the recent reader is;
- Calder's hidden role.

## Transition into the older restricted annex
The Hall of Seals should contain evidence that the oldest relevant material was **not kept in the ordinary historical collection**.

Possible reasons:
- the older chamber predates the Yahtrean archive layout;
- fragile Diysean seal pieces were preserved closer to where they were found;
- later archivists sealed a dangerous / unstable lower section rather than moving everything;
- old seal-keeper notes refer to a restricted chamber beyond the Hall whose original purpose was never fully understood.

The recent reader's trail points the same way.

The party proceeds into older architecture.

### Visual shift
The environment changes from:
- shelves;
- cases;
- desks;
- ledgers;
- preserved regalia;

into:
- broad stone passages;
- heavy carved doors;
- physical seals worked into lintels;
- old ward-lines cut into the floor and walls;

- fewer later Yahtrean additions;
- signs that the chambers beyond were used for serious civic / command functions rather than ordinary scholarship.

This is the proper transition toward the First Command Warden.

Mandatory end state:
- the recent reader's identity remains unknown;

- the trail points into older protected chambers;
