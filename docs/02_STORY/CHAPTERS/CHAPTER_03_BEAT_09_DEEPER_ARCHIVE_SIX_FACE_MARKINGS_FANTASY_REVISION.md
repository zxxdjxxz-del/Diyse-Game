# Chapter 3 — Beat 9 — Deeper Archive / Six-Face Markings

**Status:** CURRENT WORKING BEAT PACKET — FANTASY PRESENTATION REVISION

## Beat purpose
Beat 9 is the first substantial exploration sequence with the completed four-person party:
- Cyanis;
- Ilyra;
- Torren;
- Nimera.

Its job is to let the new group breathe as a real party while the Old City begins revealing the **six Faces as part of Diysean magical culture and record craft**.

This beat should:
1. provide a real exploration stretch after Nimera's recruitment;
2. show deeper Old City spaces becoming less like a modern Crown archive and more like a preserved Ancient magical archive;
3. introduce recurring physical markings for **Might, Elements, Grace, Resource, Perception, and Ruin**;
4. establish that the six marks were used repeatedly to organize knowledge, craft, study, and old magical work;
5. keep Cyanis's Card unclassified;
6. let the four permanent party members contribute differently;
7. gradually move from scholarly archive chambers toward older command galleries;
8. end at the threshold of **Beat 10 — Command Galleries / Hall of Seals**.

The Old City should feel ancient, monumental, enchanted, and handmade—not computerized.

## Area identity — an Ancient archive, not machinery rooms
The environment should become more visibly Diysean through physical magical craft.

Useful visual language:
- towering fitted stone walls with dark metal ribs;
- shelves built directly into monumental alcoves;
- sealed tablet drawers and old scroll-cases;
- engraved rings set into floors and walls;
- bronze or black-metal arms once used for copying or moving records;
- stone tables scarred by centuries of use;
- old sigils filled with faint dormant light;
- chains, rails, rotating shelves, sliding shutters, counterweights, and carved guide grooves;
- small Crown lamps and ropes looking temporary against much older construction.

When something moves, it should **turn, slide, lift, unfold, unlock, or glow** in a way the player can physically understand.

## Exploration rhythm
Recommended flow:
1. move through deeper record halls after Beat 8;
2. encounter the first set of six recurring marks;
3. return to normal gameplay and ordinary encounters;
4. find the same six marks used differently in another room;
5. stop for a short authored scene where Nimera compares the examples;
6. navigate a bounded side problem involving an old marked passage;
7. reach a larger chamber where all six Faces appear together;
8. establish the sixfold ordering as intentional;
9. continue into architecture that is less about storing knowledge and more about keeping or directing it;
10. arrive at the sealed entrance to the command galleries.

No walking exposition during ordinary traversal.

## First Six-Face discovery
The party first encounters six marks repeated in a deliberate physical arrangement.

A strong presentation would be:
- six carved emblems set above six old archive alcoves;
- each emblem paired with a distinct border pattern repeated on tablet cases and shelf faces;
- old traces of colored mineral or magical inlay still visible in the carvings;
- all six given equal weight in the room.

At first, the party only needs to establish:
- there are exactly six;
- the arrangement is deliberate;
- the marks recur on the records themselves;
- this is organization, not decoration.

Torren may notice the repeated physical placement before anyone names them.

Nimera recognizes that the marks correspond to the six Faces known in modern Card scholarship, but she does not pretend to know exactly how Diyseans understood every one of them.

---

## The six canonical Faces
The six marks correspond to:
- **Might**
- **Elements**
- **Grace**
- **Resource**
- **Perception**
- **Ruin**

Use only these current names.

The discovery should feel archaeological and magical, not like reading six labels from a panel.

A modern scholar like Nimera can identify them because:
- the symbol families survived in later Card scholarship;
- fragments of the marks already appear in known magical traditions;
- the forms recur consistently enough to compare.

What remains uncertain is how broad each Face's Ancient role was.

---

## Second occurrence — the marks belong to the place
Later, the same six Face marks appear somewhere that is **not simply shelving**.

Possible presentation:
- six sigils carved around a stone turning-ring that directs a set of heavy archive shutters;
- six marked paths inlaid into the floor leading to separate old study chambers;
- six seal impressions repeated around a large record vault door;
- six families of marks worked into the borders of old copying tables.

This shows that the Faces were woven into more than book sorting.

The group can reasonably infer that Diyseans used the six Faces as a broad way of arranging magical knowledge and practice.

They still cannot explain the entire civilization through them.

---

## Small exploration problem — a marked passage
Beat 9 should contain one local problem using the six marks without turning them into a menu puzzle.

Recommended setup:
- a broad stone passage is blocked by a set of interlocked shutters;
- six old Face seals are carved around a nearby turning ring;
- only some of the corresponding engraved channels still carry faint light;
- physical wear shows that the mechanism once opened in more than one arrangement;
- nearby record cases carry matching marks that imply which chamber those shutters once served.

The party solves it through observation:
- Torren finds the worn path and which parts still physically move;
- Nimera matches the nearby records and marks;
- Cyanis notices how the engraved sealwork balances around the ring;
- Ilyra keeps them from forcing a damaged section and chooses the safer working arrangement.

When they get it right:
- carved lines brighten;
- the ring turns with effort;
- old catches release;
- the shutters withdraw into the stone.

No disembodied voice announces success.

## Cyanis's Card remains unidentified
The Card can remain quiet through most or all of Beat 9.

If it reacts at all, the reaction must remain broad and ambiguous.

Do not have:
- Might alone glow near Cyanis;
- the Card fit a Might-shaped recess;
- a mark identify him;
- Nimera declare the Card's Face;
- the Old City name him as bearer.

The stronger identification must remain later.

---

## Ruin presentation
Ruin must appear as an ordinary, legitimate member of the sixfold set.

Do not make its mark:
- cracked;
- blackened;
- cursed-looking;
- isolated behind warnings;
- visibly hostile compared with the other five.

The visual message is simple:
> Diyseans treated Ruin as one of the six Faces, not as a corruption of the other five.

Its deeper meaning does not need to be explained yet.

## Ordinary encounters
Random encounters can now become a regular part of deeper Old City exploration.

Enemy presentation should remain physical-fantasy:
- warding constructs;
- old archive guardians;
- animated scribing arms detached from their stations;
- stone-and-metal sentries;
- maintenance devices continuing forgotten duties violently.

They do not talk like artificial intelligences.

They do not issue computer-like warnings.

Their actions are visible through posture, sigils, light, movement, bells, chimes, locking shutters, weapon-arms, or changing ward marks.

## Major Six-Face chamber
Near the latter half of Beat 9, the party reaches a larger chamber where the six Faces are unmistakably central to the room's design.

Recommended presentation:
- six great archive alcoves around a circular stone floor;
- each alcove bears one Face mark;
- a central low dais carries a six-spoked metal-and-stone inlay;
- old record cases from each alcove use matching border marks;
- worn footpaths show scholars once moved freely among all six.

This gives the party its strongest Beat-9 conclusion:
> **The six Faces were built into how at least some Diyseans organized magical knowledge and work.**

That is significant enough.

Do not turn the room into a total explanation of Diysean society.

---

## Transition toward Beat 10
Beyond the Six-Face chamber, the feel of the architecture changes.

Instead of more study rooms and archive alcoves, the party begins finding:
- heavier doors bearing old seal impressions;
- raised stone stations where keepers or officials once stood;
- paired ceremonial and mechanical levers worked into carved pillars;
- walls bearing office marks, keeper signs, and formal seals rather than archive labels;
- broad galleries built for movement of people rather than records;
- a distant sealed hall watched by old statues or dormant warding figures.

Nimera can tell this is no longer merely a scholarly archive area.

Torren can tell the route has narrowed into a deliberate controlled passage.

The party has reached the edge of a place where someone once **kept authority**, not merely knowledge.

## Beat ending
Beat 9 ends before the party enters the deeper command hall.

They now know:
- all six Faces recur throughout the Ancient archive;
- those marks were used in real magical and scholarly organization;
- Ruin belonged naturally among the six;
- Cyanis's Card remains unidentified;
- the deeper route ahead changes from archive chambers into seal-heavy command galleries;
- something more serious was guarded beyond.

Handoff → **Beat 10 — Command Galleries / Hall of Seals**.
