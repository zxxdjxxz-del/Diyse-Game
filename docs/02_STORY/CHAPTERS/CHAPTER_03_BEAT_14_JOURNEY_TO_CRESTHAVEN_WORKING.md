## Beat purpose
Beat 14 turns Mirena's Cresthaven identification into a real field move.

It should:
1. begin after Mirena tentatively identifies the exact-Crest-marked northern location on the existing Ancient map as **Cresthaven**;
2. keep Mirena's concern about internal access / powerful magic active without turning the journey into a conspiracy exposition beat;
3. establish that Mirena wants the party to investigate Cresthaven because it is the strongest physical lead they currently possess;
4. keep the playable traveling party as **Cyanis + Ilyra + Torren + Nimera**;
5. have Mirena arrange a small Crown support / supply presence and plan to meet them at Cresthaven with Maevra for the later headquarters handoff, rather than joining the permanent traveling party;
6. give the trip north enough playable space to feel like a real transition between Caelora and the next operating base;
7. present Cresthaven as an **abandoned forest fort / old Crown outpost** whose surviving Crown construction sits over or incorporates much older Diysean remains;
8. confirm that Mirena's geographic identification was correct without explaining why the Ancient map bears the exact Yahtrean Crest;
9. avoid inserting a new mandatory boss merely to fill the travel beat;
10. end with the party physically arriving at / entering Cresthaven so Beat 15 can handle the Crown handoff, minimum reopening work, and cleanup-window opening.

Beat 14 is:
> **Mirena gives the destination → permanent four travel north → Cresthaven comes into view → old map placement is confirmed → arrival.**

Exact dialogue remains deferred to the Agent-Brain rehearsal-first Dialogue Engine.

## Mirena's immediate plan
Mirena should not turn this into a large public Crown expedition.

Her concern from Beat 13 remains:
- someone involved may have meaningful access to Crown material / channels;
- someone involved has demonstrated unusually strong magic;
- the investigation may touch people or institutions that cannot safely be assumed clean.

Therefore the next move should remain deliberately small.

Mirena's plan:
- Cyanis, Ilyra, Torren, and Nimera go to Cresthaven as the field group;
- Mirena quietly authorizes access to the abandoned site;
- she arranges a **small practical Crown support group** with basic supplies, tools, lighting, bedding, medical stores, and enough personnel to reopen only essential rooms;
- Maevra remains with Mirena for the Crown side of the movement;
- Mirena and Maevra will be present for the initial Cresthaven handoff once the site is safe enough to use;
- this is not a public mobilization and should not look like a royal army occupation.

### Party-state lock
The **playable travel party remains only:**
- Cyanis;
- Ilyra;
- Torren;
- Nimera.

Mirena and Maevra do not become temporary combat members.

## Why go to Cresthaven now
Cresthaven is the best lead because several independent facts now point toward it:
- the Ancient map places a significant northern location there;
- that location bears the exact Yahtrean Crest on material far older than Yahtrea should be;
- the old route evidence continues north through / beyond that region;
- Mirena knows Cresthaven as a real abandoned Crown site in approximately the same geographic position;
- the party needs a place from which to continue investigating without routing every discovery through normal Caeloran channels.

This does **not** mean Cresthaven is known to contain the culprit or a solved answer.

The reason to go is:
> **the map says something important was there, the modern site still exists, and nobody currently understands why the Crest links them.**

## Travel presentation
The journey should be playable enough to establish distance and transition, but it should not become a second giant dungeon after the Old City.

Recommended structure:
1. depart Caelora / transition onto the northbound route;
2. a short first travel stretch re-establishes ordinary field exploration after the dense Old City interior;
3. one or more side paths / resource pockets may reward exploration under area-design authority;
4. random encounters use the current Chapter-3 regional encounter table;
5. the environment becomes increasingly wooded / less maintained as the party leaves the actively used routes behind;
6. old Crown way markers or remains of a former service road begin appearing;
7. the road narrows toward the abandoned fort site;
8. first distant / partial view of Cresthaven through the forest;
9. final approach through overgrowth and old defensive works;
10. physical arrival at the outer fort / entrance;
11. handoff into Beat 15.

### Dialogue staging rule
Do not fill the journey with constant walking dialogue.

Use the established rhythm:
> gameplay → stop for a reason → concise authored scene → gameplay.

Possible authored stops should be tied to something concrete:
- Torren comparing terrain against the old copied map;
- the party finding an old Crown marker confirming they are approaching Cresthaven;
- Nimera noticing that an exposed older stone beneath later Crown work resembles Diysean material;
- first sight of the fort.

No one needs to speak merely because they have been walking for a while.

## Route / environment identity
The trip should feel like movement toward a place the modern Crown abandoned rather than a mystical hidden kingdom.

Favor:
- maintained road giving way to an older neglected spur;
- forest reclaiming drainage ditches / retaining walls;
- mossed Crown mile markers or boundary stones;
- fallen timber / storm damage / roots pushing through old paving;
- sections where the road has narrowed to one usable line;
- old bridgework / culverts / retaining stone if geography needs them;
- signs that people once moved supplies here regularly but no longer do.

Do not make the path impossibly lost if Mirena can identify Cresthaven on modern Crown records.

The challenge is **neglect and abandonment**, not that nobody knows the fort exists.

---

## Cresthaven visual identity — abandoned forest fort
Cresthaven should arrive as a strong location reveal.

Current required identity:
> **an abandoned forest fort / former Crown outpost built over or into much older Diysean structure.**

First-arrival visual language may include:
- weathered Crown stonework / timber defenses partly swallowed by vegetation;
- an old gate or gatehouse no longer in normal service;
- walls or defensive terraces broken in places but still substantial;
- trees growing close against old fortifications;
- moss / ivy / roots over later Crown construction;
- a central keep / administration block or equivalent surviving core;
- older stone visible beneath later repairs and additions;
- strange older foundations, seams, arches, fitted blocks, or buried lower courses that do not match the Crown architecture;
- enough surviving structure that reopening a small headquarters is plausible.

Cresthaven should not look like:
- a thriving town;
- an active military base;
- a fully ruined pile with no usable rooms;
- a pristine Ancient temple;
- a magical city suddenly revealed intact.

It is a **Crown fort laid over something older**, abandoned but recoverable.

## Confirming the map match
The journey should confirm Mirena's identification through geography rather than a convenient sign explaining everything.

Torren can verify that:
- the relative direction from Caelora fits;
- terrain / approach relationships line up closely enough with the copied Ancient material;
- the marked location corresponds strongly to where Cresthaven now stands.

That is enough to say:
> **this is almost certainly the location marked on the Ancient map.**

### What remains impossible to explain
The party still cannot explain:
- why the Ancient map bears the **exact Yahtrean Crest** at this location;
- whether Cresthaven itself predates Yahtrea;
- whether the Crown unknowingly built on a site already important to the Diyseans;
- whether the Crest existed in some older form / meaning;
- whether the modern fort name preserves anything Ancient;
- what the northbound Ancient route was ultimately for.

Do not collapse that mystery on arrival.

## The deeper Diysean layer — first arrival only
Beat 14 may visually establish that Cresthaven contains older material, but it should not spend the entire next chapter's archaeology budget immediately.

Allowed first-arrival evidence:
- foundation stones too large / precise for the later fort;
- sealed lower arches;
- older wall segments incorporated into Crown construction;
- a buried stair / lower level visible but not yet explored;
- old fitted stone under a collapsed Crown floor;
- construction records / signage implying the fort reused pre-existing ruins.

Do not yet use Beat 14 to explain:
- full Diysean purpose of the site;
- why the Crest appears on the Ancient map;
- the ultimate northern-route destination;
- Last Sentinel's origin;
- the royal-seal culprit.

Cresthaven's first job is to become a **place the party can stand in and work from**.
