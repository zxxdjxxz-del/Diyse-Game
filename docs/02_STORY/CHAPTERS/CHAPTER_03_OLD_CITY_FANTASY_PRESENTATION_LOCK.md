# Chapter 3 — Old City Fantasy Presentation Lock

**Status:** ACTIVE PRESENTATION CORRECTION  
**Scope:** Chapter 3 Old City material, especially Beats 5–12  
**Purpose:** prevent Ancient Diysean spaces, magic, records, barriers, and mechanisms from reading like computers, networks, software, terminals, or modern information systems.

## Core rule
The Old City is the surviving work of an **ancient magical civilization**.

It may be extraordinarily sophisticated, precise, responsive, and mechanically complex, but its presentation must remain rooted in:
- carved stone and fitted metal;
- inscribed sigils and Face-marks;
- enchanted seals and wards;
- physical doors, shutters, rings, rails, counterweights, arms, shelves, tablets, scroll-cases, record vaults, and scribing devices;
- magical light running through engraved channels;
- old crafted mechanisms that **stir, answer, lock, turn, slide, bind, release, or awaken**;
- visible craftsmanship and ritual logic rather than invisible digital logic.

The Old City should feel closer to an impossible ancient magical archive, temple-workshop, royal vault, observatory, or enchanted civic complex than to a buried computer facility.

## Language to avoid
Do not describe Old City functions with repeated computer/network/software language such as:
- system state;
- interface;
- terminal;
- access permissions;
- credential;
- authentication token;
- classification engine;
- routing protocol;
- processing node;
- network;
- data stream;
- database;
- user;
- input/output;
- command architecture;
- response state;
- active/inactive state as if reading software flags;
- "the system recognizes" a person in a digital sense.

Writer-facing technical shorthand may occasionally exist in implementation documents, but story/development prose should not build the player's mental image out of it.

## Preferred fantasy language
Use concrete magical/physical language instead.

Examples:
- **routing** → branching passage, guide channel, marked way, turning gallery, directing seal;
- **classification** → sorting, grouping, sixfold ordering, marked family, Face tradition;
- **access** → warded door, sealed passage, barred gallery, keeper's seal, permitted chamber;
- **node** → sigil, seal, plate, carved junction, marked stone, ring;
- **response** → answer, stir, brighten, hum, shift, warm, ring, awaken;
- **condition** → required arrangement, matching marks, completed sealwork, balanced pattern;
- **processing** → scribing, sorting, copying, shelving, binding, transferring records;
- **authority** → seal, office, command mark, keeper's sign, royal/warden mark, oath-mark;
- **mechanism** remains acceptable when referring to an actual physical mechanism.

## Six Faces presentation
The six Faces may be built into Ancient record keeping and magical craft, but they should not look like six software categories in a menu.

Present them through physical repetition:
- six carved emblems above six alcoves;
- six families of border marks on record cases;
- six colored or differently shaped inlays;
- six spokes around a stone-and-metal archive ring;
- six marked shelves or vault mouths;
- six seals repeated in old diagrams;
- six traditions referenced in surviving scholarship.

The party may infer that the Faces were used to **organize knowledge and magical practice**, but the space should still look handcrafted, monumental, and old.

## Responsive magic rule
When an Ancient object answers the party, do not write it like a computer checking authorization.

Good presentation:
- a seal warms under a hand;
- engraved lines fill with light;
- an old ring turns when matching sigils are aligned;
- a ward loosens when a pattern is completed;
- a door remains stubbornly sealed when the wrong marks are brought together;
- a Warden awakens because an old command hall has been disturbed.

Avoid:
- "permission granted";
- "credential accepted";
- "route validated";
- "user recognized";
- "classification successful."

## Scribe Engine presentation correction
The **Archive Scribe Engine** remains valid as a fantasy machine name, but its presentation should be tangible and artisanal:
- articulated bronze/black-metal scribing arms;
- revolving tablets or record drums;
- cutters, styluses, stamp-seals, chain-driven shelves, enchanted inlay;
- magical marks burning or fading across plates;
- an old device violently continuing a forgotten archive duty.

It is not a robot running software.

## Beat 9 correction
Beat 9 remains **Deeper Archive / Six-Face Markings**, but computer-like phrases in the earlier packet are superseded by the revised Beat-9 authority file.

The six Faces are discovered as recurring **marks, traditions, and organizing signs** in the physical archive.

The party should feel that Diyseans built their worldview into the architecture and record craft—not that they tagged files with metadata.

## Beat 10 presentation rename
The working presentation name **Command Route / Authority Architecture** is retired.

Use:
> **Beat 10 — Command Galleries / Hall of Seals**

This beat should move from scholarly archive spaces into chambers where old Diysean officials, keepers, or wardens once exercised command through seals, marked doors, carved stations, ceremonial/mechanical controls, and physical magical authority.

It must not resemble a server room, control center, or software admin layer.

## Permanent presentation statement
> **Ancient Diyse was magically advanced, not digitally coded. Its surviving works should feel crafted, enchanted, physical, ceremonial, and architectural—even when their logic is precise enough to be studied and reproduced.**
