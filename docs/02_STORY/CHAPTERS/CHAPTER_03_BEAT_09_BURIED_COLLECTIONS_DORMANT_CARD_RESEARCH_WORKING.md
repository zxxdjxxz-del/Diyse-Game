## Beat purpose
Beat 9 becomes the first major **research-library exploration** with the completed four-person party:
- Cyanis;
- Ilyra;
- Torren;
- Nimera.

The party is not wandering deeper merely because the Old City is interesting.

They have three specific research questions:
1. **Inactive / dormant Cards** — what causes a Card that appears inactive to respond, wake, change, or produce light;
2. **the green-and-gold light Cyanis's Card emitted** — whether older Yahtrean or Ancient Diysean sources describe anything comparable;
3. **Queen Lysara's seal** — whether older sources describe royal / sovereign seals, magical seals, inherited seal traditions, or physical seals that can carry recognizable magical authority beyond a simple stamped mark.

A fourth supporting question becomes increasingly important:
4. **the location where Cyanis found the Card** — whether historical records, old maps, expedition accounts, Card studies, or Diysean texts say anything about that site.

The beat should:
- make the Old City feel like a real layered library and manuscript repository;
- let Nimera function as a scholar without becoming an exposition machine;
- make research itself playable through movement, shelf selection, physical books, manuscripts, reading tables, and copied notes;
- introduce the canonical Six Faces through scholarship / manuscripts rather than computer-like infrastructure;
- let the party find partial but meaningful information on dormant Cards and magical seal traditions;
- avoid solving the Card or Queen-seal mysteries prematurely;
- escalate when the party discovers that **someone has recently been looking through many of the same books**;
- culminate in the discovery of **still-open books / manuscripts concerning the location where Cyanis found the Card**;
- hand into Beat 10, which follows that recent research trail farther into the restricted old collections and the Hall of Seals.

Beat 9 is primarily:
> **library exploration → focused research → partial historical context → evidence of a recent unknown reader.**

It is not a machine-interface sequence.

## Location identity — the buried collections
The next major area should look and feel like a **library that has accumulated centuries of scholarship over older Diysean remains**.

This is important.

The party should move through spaces containing:
- shelves of old Yahtrean books;
- handwritten scholarly copies of older works;
- ancient Diysean manuscripts preserved in fitted cases;
- scroll tubes and folio cabinets;
- bound field journals;
- expedition logs;
- early Card treatises;
- copied diagrams;
- illuminated magical texts;
- seal / heraldry references;
- historical maps;
- damaged fragments mounted or wrapped for study;
- reading tables with weights, bookmarks, note slips, and protective cloth;
- ladders / rolling steps / narrow upper shelf walks where appropriate;
- locked cabinets for fragile or restricted works;
- old stone alcoves incorporated into later Yahtrean shelving.

The visual impression should be:
> **generations of Yahtrean scholars built a working research library inside the surviving chambers of something much older.**

Not every book is Ancient.

The mix matters:
- later Yahtrean scholars wrote about older discoveries;
- some texts preserve copies of lost Diysean originals;
- some Ancient manuscripts survive physically;
- some modern conclusions are incomplete or wrong;
- Nimera knows how to distinguish primary material from later interpretation.

---

## Why this library was not already 'solved'
The existence of old books does not mean every mystery should already be answered.

Reasons information remains incomplete:
- many texts are fragmentary;
- some Ancient terminology does not map cleanly into modern Yahtrean language;
- older Yahtrean scholars often worked from incomplete finds;
- some books contradict each other;
- some Card phenomena were rare enough to be recorded only as unusual cases;
- certain manuscripts describe results but not methods;
- seal traditions may have changed over centuries;
- some relevant collections were miscatalogued under broader subjects;
- the exact combination of **inactive Card + green/gold light + discovery site + impossible royal-seal response** has not necessarily been studied together before.

Nimera's value is not that she already knows the answer.

Her value is that she knows **where to look, how to compare sources, and how not to overstate what a damaged old text proves**.

## The four research threads

### Thread A — inactive / dormant Cards
The party searches material on Cards that:
- were found inert;
- failed to respond for long periods;
- changed appearance later;
- reacted only under unusual circumstances;
- were considered damaged but later proved responsive;
- were described as 'sleeping,' 'silent,' 'closed,' 'unawakened,' or equivalent period language where appropriate.

The texts do not need to use one perfectly standardized modern term.

Useful partial findings:
- an inactive Card is not always a dead or ruined Card;
- some Cards historically changed state after long periods of no visible response;
- scholars disagreed over whether the cause was the Card, the person, place, magic around it, or some combination;
- records exist of Cards producing light or geometric change before their nature was understood;
- surviving descriptions are often frustratingly imprecise about exact color.

### Mandatory limit
Do not let the library simply contain:
> 'Green and gold means this exact Card is Last Sentinel.'

The research should strengthen the idea that Cyanis's Card may have been **inactive rather than broken**, but not classify it yet.

---

### Thread B — green-and-gold light
This should be one of the party's most concrete search terms because it comes from firsthand observation.

Possible materials:
- old Card-study journals;
- hand-painted marginal illustrations of luminous Card phenomena;
- descriptions of colored halos / veins / filaments / borders;
- accounts of Cards changing light as they move between states;
- copied Diysean notes where color carried significance but translation is uncertain;
- later Yahtrean scholars debating whether green/gold descriptions referred to one phenomenon or several.

Useful result:
- **green-and-gold light is not unheard of in the record**, but the surviving references are too varied or incomplete to give a clean identification.

The party may find several descriptions that are uncomfortably close to what Cyanis saw, enough to justify continuing.

### Color caution
Do not let color alone identify a Face or Prime class.

The old material can make clear that:
- color may indicate state, reaction, condition, or surrounding magic;
- the same broad color language may appear in unrelated Card accounts;
- later stable coloration may matter more than an early transient glow.

This protects the later **deep Ruby** stabilization and subsequent identification sequence.

---

### Thread C — Queen Lysara's seal / magical seal traditions
The party must actively research the Queen-seal mystery too.

They should look for material on:
- old Yahtrean royal seals;
- personal sovereign seals;
- magical authentication traditions;
- seals that carry more than a visible impression;
- inherited royal objects;
- ceremonial seals versus working seals;
- old records of seals being renewed, repaired, blessed, charged, bound, or otherwise maintained;
- Ancient Diysean use of seals / signets / authority marks where such material survives.

### What Beat 9 may establish
The library can provide historical support for several bounded conclusions:
- Yahtrea has a long tradition of treating certain royal seals as **magically meaningful objects**, not merely engraved stamps;
- older sources sometimes distinguish the physical mark from the magical authority associated with it;
- some historical seals were maintained through specialized magical craft no longer commonly practiced or fully understood;
- fragments suggest that much older Diysean culture also used physical seals / marks in serious civic, military, or administrative contexts;
- the exact relationship between those Ancient practices and Queen Lysara's current personal seal remains uncertain.

### What Beat 9 must NOT establish
Do not yet prove:
- Queen Lysara's current physical seal is an Ancient Diysean artifact;
- her seal has survived unchanged from the Diysean era;
- the exact method by which magical authority is placed into a seal;
- that someone can reproduce its response remotely;
- who caused the suspect Card orders to bear Lysara's genuine magical authority;
- Calder's hidden transport role;
- that the Card and royal seal use the same magical principle.

Those stronger discoveries belong later, especially Beat 12's Ancient seal-working chamber.

The library should tell the party:
> **the impossible seal behavior has historical precedent as a kind of problem worth asking about — but not yet how it happened this time.**

---

### Thread D — the place where Cyanis found the Card
The party should also search for the location itself.

Do not invent a new place name in this packet if current authority has not supplied one.

Use the known site as the research key.

Possible source types:
- old regional maps;
- excavation notes;
- travel accounts;
- Crown surveys;
- Card-recovery reports;
- ruined-site inventories;
- Ancient fragments associated with the area;
- old scholarly arguments over what the site originally was.

The first important result does not need to be a huge lore answer.

It can simply establish that the site has appeared in older scholarship more often than the party expected.

That makes the later recent-reader evidence far more unsettling.

Nimera leads the party toward older scholarship and copied Diysean material.

The space becomes:

Here the Six-Face material can appear naturally in:
- old Card diagrams;

- chapter headings;
- seals on manuscript cases;

### Section 3 — seals and sovereign objects
The party searches Yahtrean history / magical craft references relevant to Queen Lysara.

This section can include:
- books on royal regalia;
- historical seal impressions preserved on sample sheets;

- accounts of older sovereign seals being renewed or maintained;

Result:
- royal-seal magic has deeper history than the present court fully discusses in ordinary procedure;
- the exact technique remains missing / fragmentary.

### Section 4 — site references
The group follows citations from Card studies into maps / regional histories concerning the place where Cyanis found the Card.

This is where the beat begins to change from ordinary research into investigation.

## Six Faces — discovered through books, not machinery
Beat 9 can still advance the Six-Face evidence trail, but the presentation must now be **scholarly and physical**.

The canonical six names are:
- **Might**;
- **Elements**;
- **Grace**;
- **Resource**;
- **Perception**;
- **Ruin**.

They may appear as:
- six repeated emblems in old Card treatises;
- headings dividing a manuscript into six related bodies of study;
- marginal notation copied from Diysean originals;
- old case-seals marking which kind of Card material is inside;
- illustrations showing six symbolic traditions arranged together;
- later Yahtrean scholarship using current names to discuss older marks.

### What the party may conclude
- the six Faces are old;
- they were important enough to appear repeatedly in preserved Card scholarship;
- older Diysean and later Yahtrean traditions are related somehow;
- modern understanding may be incomplete.

### What they may NOT conclude
- Cyanis's Card is Might;
- Torren is 'recognized' as Perception;
- Ruin is evil;
- every Ancient magical practice fits neatly into the modern six-Face model;
- modern terminology is a perfect translation of Diysean thought.

Ruin should appear as one legitimate category among six, with no sinister visual treatment.

## The research should produce partial answers, not empty shelves
The party needs to feel that the library search matters.

By the time they have worked through the first three research threads, they should have gained real progress:

### On the Card
They can reasonably believe:
- long-inactive Cards have sometimes become responsive again;
- visible light / geometric change can accompany that process;
- Cyanis's Card is not behaving in a way the historical record would dismiss as impossible;
- green/gold descriptions exist, though they are not enough to identify the Card.

### On the Queen's seal
They can reasonably believe:
- Yahtrean royal-seal magic has older roots than a simple court authentication custom;
- historical sources distinguish a seal's physical impression from the magical authority bound to / carried through it;
- older maintenance / renewal practices existed;
- there may be older Diysean precedent for magical seals or civic authority marks;
- they need older, more specialized material to understand how such magic was actually placed or maintained.

### On the discovery site
They can reasonably believe:
- the site where Cyanis found the Card has appeared in historical / scholarly records before;
- some writers connected it to unusual Card finds or older ruins, but the evidence is incomplete.

These are meaningful answers while still preserving the chapter's larger mysteries.

## Escalation — someone has been here recently
The party gradually notices that some of the material they need has already been disturbed.

This should not begin as an obvious crime scene.

At first it can look like normal scholarly use.

Signs accumulate:
- a book shelved incorrectly but recently dusted around its outline;
- a stack left on a side table instead of returned;
- paper weights still holding pages open;
- recent bookmark slips / thread markers;
- several volumes from different subject areas gathered together in one reading space;
- one fragile manuscript case not fully reset after use;
- fresh scuffing around a ladder or shelf stool;
- a lamp recently used or replaced where that is plausible;
- copied references pulled from Card shelves, seal history, and regional maps.

### Torren's role
Torren should be especially useful here.

He may notice:
- the disturbance is recent rather than decades old;
- the same reader appears to have moved between otherwise unrelated collections;
- books were selected with purpose rather than randomly browsed;
- the research path overlaps the party's own questions.

He cannot identify the person from footprints / dust unless current authority later gives specific evidence.

### Nimera's role
Nimera can identify:
- which books are normally shelved where;
- which collections are rarely consulted;
- whether the combination of subjects is unusual;
- whether someone appears to have followed citations from one book into another.

She should not automatically know who used the table merely because it is her workplace.

---

## The recent reader was tracing the same cluster of questions
The crucial realization is not simply:
> someone has been in the library.

It is:
> **someone has recently been researching a disturbingly similar set of subjects.**

The gathered / disturbed works connect:
- inactive Cards;
- unusual luminous Card behavior;
- older seal traditions / magical authority objects;
- regional records relating to the place where Cyanis found the Card.

This does **not** prove the unknown researcher knew about Cyanis personally.

It does prove the same subjects were being connected before the party arrived here.

---

## Beat-ending discovery — open books on the Card's discovery location
The strongest evidence should be physical and immediate.

Near the end of Beat 9, the party reaches a reading table / study alcove deeper in the collection.

There they find:
- books and manuscripts **still open**;
- at least one old map / site account / historical record concerning **the location where Cyanis found the Card**;
- nearby Card-study material dealing with inactive / responsive Cards;
- another volume or set of notes connected to magical seals / sovereign authority traditions;
- page markers linking several passages together.

The scene should look like someone **left in the middle of active research**, not like an ancient tableau.

### Important ambiguity
Do not automatically make the room abandoned seconds ago.

The evidence can establish **recent use** without forcing an immediate chase.

Do not include:
- a still-warm cup unless the story specifically needs the researcher only minutes ahead;
- a conveniently dropped personal insignia;
- a signed note identifying the researcher;
- Calder's name;
- proof the unknown researcher authored the Card transport orders.

The purpose is to create a live human mystery, not solve it cheaply.
