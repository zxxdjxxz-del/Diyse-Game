## Character core
Warm, bold, emotionally direct, energetic, stubborn, socially fearless, generous, observant, physically expressive, irreverent in private, and more impulsive than Mirena or Alaric.

Her first instinct is often to ask:
> **Who is hurting?**

Her broader pressure question is:
> **Who is paying for this right now?**

She likes people easily and is emotionally easier to read than the other Ceryths without being naive or immature.

Humanitarian concern is material rather than ceremonial:
- food;
- shelter;
- transport;
- safety;
- evacuation;
- information;
- recovery;
- dignity.

## Upbringing and Cassian legacy
Nalia was not born a symbolic humanitarian. She was a lively royal child who noticed discomfort, exclusion, fear, and who was being ignored.

Cassian taught her to ask the second question: not just what someone needs now, but how long the need lasts, who else is affected, and what continues tomorrow.

Compatible lessons include:
- compassion should become useful rather than identity-performance;
- never promise what the Crown cannot deliver;
- helping can be boring logistics;
- dignity matters as much as provision;
- deprivation is not solidarity.

Lysara taught that feelings are real but are not automatically instructions.

Cassian's death intensified Nalia's instinct to run toward need and created a dangerous false lesson:
> if someone needs help and she can help, she should personally be the one who goes.

Healthy inheritance:
> help because she chooses to, not because she must repay Cassian's death or prove that she understood him.

## Adult public role
Serious civilian role across:
- relief;
- displacement response;
- recovery coordination;
- humanitarian access;
- family reunification;
- evacuation;
- temporary housing;
- essential-supply distribution;
- related civilian support.

Exact office title remains **OPEN**.

Central principle:
> receiving help should not require surrendering dignity.

Aid should preserve, where possible:
- clear information;
- privacy;
- choice;
- fair access;
- freedom from performing gratitude;
- freedom from surrendering personal stories as the price of assistance.

Royal presence is a tool, not aid. Mature Nalia asks whether her presence will actually improve the situation before appearing.

She values local expertise but does not romanticize it: local officials, workers, and recipients may disagree, and local authority is not automatically correct.

## Character pressure
Care must not become:
- intrusion;
- personal indispensability;
- the assumption that help grants access;
- self-burning treated as proof of compassion.

A mature Nalia asks:
> **Do you want help?**

And allows **No** to remain a complete answer.

## Ordinary/private life
Private Nalia is faster, louder, more profane, more physically expressive, and much sillier than her public humanitarian role might suggest.

She can:
- tell exaggerated stories recreationally while remaining accurate in serious reports;
- joke spontaneously;
- use adult/dirty humor;
- become socially overenthusiastic;
- get visibly irritated over low-stakes things;
- enjoy food and ordinary comforts without treating deprivation as moral virtue;
- need rest, privacy, and a life not measured by usefulness.

Her **hangry** tendency is active character texture: hunger reduces patience and can produce ridiculous low-stakes irritability.

## Voice and state behavior
Nalia speaks toward the person inside the problem.

- **Public voice:** concrete, warm, politically literate.
- **Private voice:** faster, louder, more profane, expressive, and sillier.
- humor is spontaneous, social, expressive, and occasionally dirty;
- anger is visibly hot, especially when suffering is deliberately depersonalized;
- because royal anger carries power, being right does not authorize humiliating subordinates;
- fear sends her toward the problem;
- guilt makes her mentally leave moments of rest before her body follows;
- difficult vulnerability is limitation rather than love: admitting she cannot fix something, needs to go home, or has nothing left today.

Growth rule:
> Care is not measured by how much of yourself you can burn.
