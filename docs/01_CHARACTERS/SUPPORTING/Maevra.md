# Maevra Solmar

**Status:** ACTIVE SUPPORTING-CHARACTER AUTHORITY  
**Domain rule:** this folder owns character identity, biography, chronology, personality, relationship logic, ordinary-life behavior, mannerisms, and concise voice direction. Exact gameplay mechanics live in their owning system domains; exact visual authority lives in `14_ART_AND_VISUALS`.

## Identity
- Full name: **Maevra Solmar**
- Age: **41**
- Role: recurring allied Yahtrean commander
- Party status: **fully non-playable supporting NPC; never a guest party member and never a seventh permanent member**
- Origin: Caeloran
- Torren chronology: joined/trained in Royal Army life at 16 while Torren was 17, 25 years before Ch0

**Naming lock:** Solmar is Maevra's canonical surname. Dialogue may use `Maevra`, `Solmar`, `Maevra Solmar`, or earned personal shorthand according to relationship, rank/formality, and scene context.

## Playability lock
Maevra may travel with, command around, rescue, brief, reinforce, or otherwise intersect with the protagonists in authored story scenes, but she does **not** enter the commandable battle formation at any point.

Her combat competence may be represented through narrative staging, allied forces, cutscene action, or command outcomes rather than a guest-party kit.

## Chapter 1 temporary injury state
Before Chapter 1 begins, Maevra's arm is **broken during the Broken Convoy attack**.

By her first Chapter-1 appearance at Brackenwall:
- the arm has already been set;
- she is wearing a practical field splint;
- the injury is still painful and materially limits combat use of the arm;
- this is the immediate in-world reason she travels with the group but does not fight during Chapter 1.

Do not invent left/right laterality unless separately locked.

Ilyra may check, change, or re-secure the splint and may use restrained healing/Grace magic to ease the pain. **Magic cannot mend the broken bone itself.** The splint therefore remains part of Maevra's Chapter-1 state through Hollow Watch, Southern Briar, the Junction, and Junction camp unless later authority explicitly changes recovery timing.

This temporary injury state does not alter the global playability lock above.

## Character core
Maevra is confident, energetic, observant, curious, socially capable, decisive, argumentative, mischievous, attentive, and warm.

She genuinely likes people. Her professional commander mode is not her entire personality, and familiarity with the group should increasingly make that obvious.

She is not a tough-command exterior waiting for a rare scene to reveal a softer hidden self. The commander and the friend are both genuinely Maevra. Which one is foregrounded depends on circumstance.

## Command philosophy
She values clarity, responsibility, competence, accountability, adaptability, and reliable delegation.

She does not worship procedure. A reasonable rule can become obsolete when evidence changes. Under pressure she gets shorter and clearer rather than theatrically louder.

A direct operational order remains a real order even when delivered to people she knows and loves. Friendship does not weaken her authority; authority does not require emotional distance.

## Familiarity versus rank — Cyanis and Ilyra
Maevra already knows **Cyanis and Ilyra very well as separate people** before the early main-game chapters. Their familiarity does **not** begin when the player meets them together.

This means Maevra may already know:
- how Cyanis tends to take responsibility onto himself;
- how he argues when he thinks an order is wrong;
- what kinds of jokes or needling will actually land with him;
- how Ilyra works under pressure;
- how Ilyra can become stubborn about another person's limits while neglecting her own;
- each person's ordinary habits, temperament, and tells to a degree consistent with prior familiarity.

She may therefore:
- tease either of them naturally;
- anticipate a likely objection;
- use shorthand instead of explaining every premise;
- know when a `fine` or `I'm all right` answer is probably incomplete;
- make a joke that only works because she knows the person receiving it;
- show warmth or irritation that clearly predates the current journey.

But **familiarity does not erase rank**.

In Chapters 0–2 especially:
- Maevra is still a senior commander over Cyanis and Ilyra in operational contexts;
- a direct order from her is not merely a friendly suggestion;
- Cyanis and Ilyra can argue, question, or push back because she respects competent disagreement, but they still recognize when she has made the call;
- Maevra does not have to become stiff or distant to preserve authority;
- off-duty familiarity and professional hierarchy can exist in the same relationship without contradiction;
- the dialogue should not treat them as instant new best friends, because the personal familiarity is already established **before** the story—what is new is the specific crisis/journey, not basic acquaintance.

The correct early read is:
> **They know one another well. Maevra is still their commander.**

This is distinct from Torren/Maevra, whose much longer private history has its own reveal boundaries.

## Group comfort / friendship progression
Maevra's relationship to the **group as a social unit** should loosen over time even though her separate familiarity with Cyanis and Ilyra already exists.

The early chapters place her in commander-heavy circumstances. That should not freeze her there permanently.

### Early Chapters 1–2
Maevra is still frequently operating as the senior commander because the situations actually demand it.

Even here, the player should already glimpse the person underneath the command posture through:
- dry jokes;
- personal teasing;
- familiar profanity;
- irritation that is obviously not formal disciplinary anger;
- knowing glances and shorthand;
- ordinary preferences;
- willingness to be teased back when the situation is safe enough.

She can be personally close to Cyanis and Ilyra while still making the final call.

### As the party settles into a real social group
Maevra should increasingly relax **with the whole group**, not merely remain the person who briefs them and then leaves the room.

She may:
- sit with them after the work is done;
- start a joke instead of only allowing one;
- gossip;
- complain about food, armor, baths, travel, paperwork, or somebody's habits;
- become invested in a stupid contest;
- argue about a bad book or melodramatic serial;
- sing badly if the mood earns it;
- drink socially;
- lose a trivial argument and keep fighting it anyway;
- tell an embarrassing or ridiculous story that has no command purpose;
- ask about somebody's life because she is interested as a friend, not because she needs an operational answer;
- be the one who gets dragged into nonsense and then becomes more competitive than everybody expected;
- stay in the room after there is no professional reason left for her to stay.

She should become an **actual friend** to the party, not a recurring authority figure who happens to exchange witty lines with them.

### Rank never disappears
Her increasing ease must not flatten her into a buddy-peer in moments of real command responsibility.

When a crisis begins:
- posture can change;
- language can tighten;
- joking may stop instantly;
- the group recognizes that Commander Solmar has made the call.

When the crisis ends, she does not need to remain in command register out of habit.

The contrast is part of the character:
> **Maevra can tell Cyanis to sit down and shut up as his commander in one scene, then lose an argument with him about a terrible novel that evening. Both are authentic.**

## Family background
Her family background is established as respected public/military service rather than one of Caelora's highest noble houses.

Existing compatible authority preserves:
- mother **Irena** — retired magistrate;
- father — Royal Army quartermaster; died from ordinary illness, not conspiracy/war.

The family surname is **Solmar**. Do not invent alternate family names from retired migration branches.

## Ordinary-life texture
Maevra has a fully adult off-duty life.

These interests should increasingly become visible around the party as trust, safety, and downtime allow. They are not trivia confined to a character sheet.

### Bad popular fiction
She enjoys bad serialized adventures, melodramatic romances, obvious mysteries, and other gloriously unserious popular fiction. She knows when it is bad and becomes invested anyway. She dislikes spoilers.

### Competition
She is not generally obsessed with winning, but once somebody explicitly turns something into a contest, she cares. Cards, darts, target games, guessing games, and stupid camp challenges can all activate this.

### Cooking
Competent and practical. Good with limited ingredients, feeding several people, knowing when food is done, and avoiding waste. She is not automatically the designated party cook.

### Music
She likes music, knows songs, and sings badly. Use lightly rather than turning it into a running gimmick.

### Baths
She genuinely values a proper hot bath after armor, marching, and filth. This is a preference, not a defining trait.

### Gossip
She is interested in people and sometimes that becomes gossip. Strong boundaries remain around confidences, sensitive military information, and deliberate humiliation.

### Small repairs
She likes fixing bounded ordinary problems: loose buckles, bent clasps, torn straps, hinges, chipped handles. Identify it, fix it, done.

### Alcohol
She drinks socially, not heavily as a habit. On rare genuinely safe/off-duty occasions she can get properly drunk.

### Embarrassment
She is difficult to embarrass about competence, sex, age, appearance, or ordinary teasing. Sentimentality being observed—the tiny exact way she loves somebody—is much more effective.

## Mannerisms and physical behavior
Compatible recurring behavior includes:
- squares objects and aligns documents unconsciously;
- stands when decisions are made;
- plans late into the night;
- deliberate exhale before disobeying an order she once would have followed;
- command posture is upright and controlled;
- field posture is wider and more actively weight-shifted;
- social posture is looser, with comfortable seating, actual smiles, raised eyebrows, and cups/books/games in hand;
- mischievous Maevra may change very little facially, which is part of why it works;
- exhaustion lowers her shoulders, heavies the eyes, and reduces verbal filtering;
- private/off-duty presentation may be partial armor or simpler clothing where appropriate.

Surname/rank-specific address is valid when supported by the current relationship and institutional context; do not mechanically reproduce obsolete rank/address habits from older branches.

## Torren relationship
Torren and Maevra:
- became friends and lovers in their late teens;
- have no betrayal/cheating/hatred retcon;
- still love one another in the present;
- have an adult reconnection arc, not a first-love arc.

Their mature principle is:
> movement and return, not confinement.

Personal address progression may use **Torren / Maevra → T / Mae** where earned by context; surnames remain available in formal/professional contexts.

## Wider relationship behavior
- **Cyanis** — established personal familiarity under a real command hierarchy; she knows his habits well enough to tease and anticipate him, respects adaptability, dislikes his tendency to absorb responsibility too quickly, and can increasingly interact with him as a genuine friend whenever command duty is not foregrounded.
- **Ilyra** — established personal familiarity and professional trust under a real command hierarchy; they can be warm, sharp, familiar, amused, gossipy, tired, or profane together without pretending Maevra has ceased to be the senior commander.
- **Torren** — long adult history, love, shared competence, teasing, and disagreement without reducing either to romance.
- **Nimera** — begins as a new younger specialist rather than instant family/friendship; Maevra's warmth, curiosity, humor, and respect can build a real independent friendship over time without turning into another command/mentor-only relationship.
- **later permanent-party members** — Maevra should form relationships with them as people, not merely evaluate them as assets or subordinates.
- **Mirena/Lysara** — authority can recognize authority while still arguing about evidence, pace, and consequence.
- She must retain independent colleagues, friends, reading, games, work, and ordinary interests outside the permanent cast.

## State behavior
- **command stress:** shorter, clearer, more task-focused;
- **exhaustion:** reduced verbal filter, looser physical control, later-night planning can become hiding in work;
- **affection:** warmth often emerges through teasing, practical attention, ordinary company, and small observed details rather than declarations;
- **relaxed friendship:** more profanity, gossip, pointless arguments, competition, casual curiosity, and willingness to remain socially present after the job is done;
- **anger:** controlled and direct until personal stakes make the polish less useful;
- **mischief:** may be almost expressionless.

## Voice shorthand
Socially flexible, observant, quick, capable of dry command humor. Formal command language increases when she is guarded or trying to regain control. Off duty she can be quicker, more profane, competitive, gossipy, affectionate, and very funny without becoming a different character.

As party friendship grows, off-duty Maevra should become easier to hear more often. Do not reserve that version of her for one rare Character-Life scene.

## Dialogue-assignment guardrail
Do not default Maevra to:
- command summary;
- tactical recap;
- mission authorization;
- responsible-adult correction;
- exposition because she is the senior officer;
- ending the conversation merely because she has the highest rank present.

Those remain valid when she genuinely owns them.

She may also speak because:
- something is funny;
- she wants gossip;
- she has a petty opinion;
- she is competitive;
- she hates a book and needs everyone to know why;
- someone insulted her taste;
- she is bored;
- she wants another drink;
- she has known the people in the room long enough to enjoy annoying them;
- she simply wants to stay and talk after the work is done.

**Command is not a dialogue assignment.**

## Anti-drift
Do not reduce Maevra to:
- commander-only personality;
- Torren's romance function;
- stern older woman with no playfulness;
- flawless authority figure;
- martyr who proves care only through work;
- supporting NPC who exists only when the party needs orders;
- stranger slowly becoming friends with Cyanis or Ilyra when the story authority says she already knows them well;
- buddy-peer whose rank disappears because the dialogue is familiar;
- permanent `responsible adult` who watches everyone else have fun;
- character whose friendship with the party never progresses beyond witty professional banter.
