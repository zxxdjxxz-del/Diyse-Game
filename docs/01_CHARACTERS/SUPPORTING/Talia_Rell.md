## Identity

## Character core
Sharp, self-controlled, observant, resilient, dryly funny, intensely protective, emotionally disciplined, practical, proud, and tender without advertising it.

She remembers pre-invasion home and their parents much more fully than Seyrik does. She is not obligated to become his memory archive.

At eleven, Talia was still a child—not a substitute parent or secret resistance mastermind. She learned to hide fear partly because a frightened five-year-old Seyrik did not need more fear handed back to him.

Black Host survival sharpened her ability to observe rules, distinguish real constraints from arbitrary coercion, and survive choices made under pressure. Survival itself is not moral contamination.

Key adult correction:
> Seyrik is her brother, not an unfinished rescue.

## Independent adult life
Talia's life exists before and outside Seyrik's return.

She must have:
- a home;

- coworkers and friends;
- plans;
- humor;
- ordinary pleasures;
- adult social life;
- boundaries that do not require a trauma explanation.

Strong professional lane:
- displaced-person records;
- supply distribution;
- housing placement;
- family tracing;
- transport/recovery logistics.

Exact job title may remain flexible until current story integration requires it.

She is employed because she is competent, not because she is a victim, symbol, or therapist.

## Central pressure
She is better at giving help than receiving it and can use work as a hiding place when distressed.

Central flaw:
> she can confuse being needed with being safe to exist.

Deep fear:
> failing someone in the exact moment they believed she would protect them.

Central contradiction:
> she fiercely protects other people's agency but can become quietly controlling when frightened.

## Seyrik boundary
Love does not create automatic:
- forgiveness;
- access;
- family restoration;
- reconciliation;
- ownership of Talia's memories.

Seyrik's remorse does not create an obligation for her, and Talia is not the court of Seyrik's worth.

She may receive an apology without comforting him or granting forgiveness. Self-hatred cannot substitute for specific accountability.

Neither sibling owns the other's memories. Adult Seyrik must eventually be able to disagree with her so they can become siblings rather than protector-and-child.

A healthy endpoint can be limited but real adult contact: letters, visits, meals, laughter, and boundaries rather than restoration of childhood closeness.

A boundary is not punishment.

## Voice and acting
Plain, precise, adult, direct, and economical. Not formal by default, not poetic, and not a female version of Seyrik.

She dislikes vague language that hides responsibility and does not overexplain a boundary. "No" may be complete.

Humor is deadpan and observational, often funniest when she appears uninterested in earning the laugh. Adult dirty humor and ordinary profanity are allowed.

Fear makes her functional. A subtle tell is checking things that have already been checked.

During difficult conversation she may keep mending, sorting, or working until emotion penetrates the practical shield. When Talia's hands stop working, pay attention.

Deep vulnerability often appears when useful language fails rather than through a polished speech.

Talia shorthand:
> She says exactly what she means until what she means becomes emotionally dangerous. Then she gets practical.
