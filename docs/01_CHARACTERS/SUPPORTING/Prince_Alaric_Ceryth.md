## Character core
Thoughtful, composed, quietly funny, methodical, socially capable, patient, observant, dependable, mildly self-deprecating, and more stubborn than his easy manner initially suggests.

He genuinely does not want Mirena's crown and does not resent absolute primogeniture. Succession speculation is unpleasant because every hypothetical promotion begins with loss of family.

Deep desire:
> to matter without having to become the center, and to choose responsibilities rather than merely become available for whichever gap appears.

Central personal flaw:
> he makes himself easy to rely on and difficult to notice needing anything.

He can resent sacrifices nobody asked him to make.

Deep insecurity:
> perhaps he is most valued when making other people's plans work.

## Upbringing and family life
Before Lysara's accession, Alaric had a privileged but recognizably familial childhood with rules, lessons, sibling misbehavior, arguments, and Lysara/Cassian as parents rather than symbols.

Young Alaric already wanted to understand why rules and systems existed. Adult composure is learned; childhood Alaric could be argumentative, stubborn, competitive, and easily offended by bad logic.

Lysara's accession taught him that institutions change human relationships even when the people themselves remain the same.

Cassian recognized Alaric becoming too convenient and repeatedly asked what Alaric himself wanted.

Cassian's death intensified Alaric's instinct to become useful so the family had one less problem. Professionally he learned that no essential system should depend on one irreplaceable good person, then failed to apply the lesson to himself.
