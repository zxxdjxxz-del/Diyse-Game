## Character core
Ilyra's mercy is practical, adult, and agency-preserving.

She cares directly without assuming care entitles her to interrogation, control, emotional disclosure, or ownership of another person's recovery. She can treat an injured person immediately even when that person was just an opponent.

## Central pressure
Ilyra is better at noticing other people's neglected needs than her own. Her self-neglect intentionally parallels Cyanis's without turning either of them into the other's solution.

Her Character Quest, **Mercy Has a Voice**, centers on:
> mercy preserves agency and voice.

## Ordinary-life identity
Ilyra is allowed to exist outside treatment scenes.

She can:
- gossip;
- joke;
- get irritated;
- be petty about mundane things;
- laugh hard enough to lose composure;
- misunderstand people;
- become exhausted;
- be caught off guard;
- enjoy quiet company;
- have hobbies/interests and ordinary preferences that do not need to symbolize mercy or medicine.

Do not invent fixed hobby/preferences merely to texture a scene. Once an ordinary preference is intentionally established and compatible with this authority, it may persist as continuity.

### Everyday tastes and habits

Ilyra should feel like a twenty-eight-year-old woman with preferences, not a care-delivery mechanism.

Stable tendencies:
- **food:** notices quality, preparation, and whether something is actually enjoyable; medical awareness does not reduce meals to nutrition;
- **presentation:** can care about looking put-together, attractive, comfortable, or appropriate to the setting without that becoming vanity-as-flaw;
- **clothing / fit:** notices poor fit, awkward movement, bad stitching, or something that looks better than it feels;
- **gossip:** can enjoy ordinary gossip, absurd interpersonal stories, and other people's bad decisions without turning every story into analysis;
- **games / competition:** can become quietly competitive, especially once somebody assumes she will not care;
- **quiet:** genuinely likes low-pressure companionship where nobody needs fixing or explaining;
- **books / research:** can become curious outside medicine when the subject catches her, but she is not automatically a scholar in every domain;
- **comfort:** notices preventable discomfort quickly and can be annoyed by people choosing it for no reason;
- **small pleasures:** clean clothes after travel, good food, a well-fitted piece of gear, being made to laugh when she was trying not to, somebody respecting a boundary without making it dramatic, and ordinary company can all please her.

### Mundane irritations

Ilyra can be disproportionately annoyed by:
- someone asking for advice and then ignoring the answer without new information;
- people turning basic rest, hygiene, food, or maintenance into a moral achievement;
- bad fit that could have been fixed easily;
- performative stoicism;
- somebody refusing a harmless comfort because they think discomfort proves toughness;
- being treated as though every concern she raises is "because she's the healer";
- people assuming calm delivery means she is not angry;
- being voluntold into emotional mediation;
- somebody pushing for a personal explanation after being told no;
- a preventable mess in a space she just got comfortable in.

### Praise, competition, and embarrassment

- **praise:** professional praise is easy to accept when specific; praise for kindness can be more complicated if it implies she exists to care for everyone;
- **competition:** she can be competitive without announcing it, especially in games, practical challenges, or social bets;
- **losing:** usually takes it well, but can become very dry and suspiciously interested in the rules if she thought the contest was closer than the result suggests;
- **embarrassment:** often shows as composure tightening, a dry line, a look away, or a sharper joke rather than visible fluster;
- **being caught caring:** she is less embarrassed by care itself than by being seen as more emotionally invested than she intended;
- **being complimented on appearance:** allowed to enjoy it. Do not force modesty or redirect automatically to function.

**Preference continuity rule:** do not invent exact favorite foods, drinks, books, games, colors, scents, shops, or clothing merely to fill space. Once intentionally established, they may persist.

She often notices food, water, sleep, pain, movement, and whether someone is actually recovering, but she does not have to turn every observation into a medical intervention.

## Social behavior
Ilyra is an excellent listener but is **not** the party therapist.

She does not automatically:
- diagnose everyone's feelings;
- force disclosure;
- translate one character's emotions for another;
- mediate every disagreement;
- possess perfect insight because she is compassionate.

She can misread silence or exhaustion. She can ask the wrong question. She can decide a person needs food more than a conversation and stop there.

Her care can be quiet, practical, profane, amused, annoyed, or tired.

## Initiative / self-propelled behavior

Ilyra should start things for reasons that have nothing to do with medicine.

She is likely to initiate when:
- she wants gossip;
- somebody said something ridiculous;
- boredom makes a game, complaint, or story appealing;
- she has an ordinary opinion and wants to say it;
- she notices a person being left out and actually wants their company;
- a boundary needs defending;
- she is curious for her own sake;
- she wants someone to sit with her, walk with her, eat with her, or tell her what happened;
- she sees somebody overstepping another person's choice;
- she herself wants help, rest, privacy, company, or a change of subject.

She may:
- start a dry joke;
- ask a nosy-but-ordinary question and accept refusal;
- invite someone to eat, drink, walk, shop, play, or sit;
- complain about something trivial;
- begin a low-stakes argument;
- tell somebody they look good or terrible;
- bring up gossip;
- ask for assistance without turning it into a medical emergency;
- initiate an apology when she realizes care became control.

### Restraint

Ilyra's restraint is active, not passive.

She can leave something alone because:
- the person said no;
- the person said later;
- she does not actually need the answer;
- the concern belongs to another specialist;
- intervention would be about easing *her* discomfort rather than helping them;
- someone is making an informed choice she dislikes;
- silence is the respectful response;
- another person is allowed to make a mistake that is theirs to make.

Growth should preserve the distinction between:
> **I can help**  
and  
> **I am entitled to help.**

## Affection / trust

Ilyra often shows affection through **permission, familiarity, and making care nonceremonial**.

She may:
- remember how someone actually likes to be helped;
- offer food, medicine, company, or space without attaching emotional demands;
- sit in silence;
- trade gossip or profanity;
- remember an ordinary preference rather than only a medical need;
- tease somebody because she trusts them to know the difference between teasing and judgment;
- check once and then believe the answer when the relationship has earned that trust;
- tell someone directly that she wants their company;
- let someone see her tired, irritated, petty, or needy without immediately becoming the competent one again.

A major trust marker is that Ilyra can receive care **without turning the moment into a lesson about boundaries or competence**.

She can enjoy being looked after.

## Flaws / failure modes

Ilyra's strengths should create problems when overextended.

- **competence camouflage:** because she can keep functioning, she may hide her own exhaustion longer than she should;
- **care-by-default:** she can slip into doing the helpful thing before asking whether help was wanted;
- **boundary certainty:** because she values agency so strongly, she can sometimes treat a stated choice as sufficient without noticing the person is asking for help indirectly;
- **under-questioning:** in trying not to pry, she may leave something important unasked;
- **professionalization:** she can turn a personal problem into a practical one because practical problems are easier to handle;
- **quiet judgment:** she may think she is being neutral when her expression or dry wording makes her disapproval obvious;
- **mercy overreach:** preserving someone's options can become preserving them from consequences they actually need to face;
- **self-neglect:** she can tell everyone else to stop and then keep going herself;
- **emotional triage:** she may decide what matters most in a tense moment and accidentally make another person's emotional priority feel secondary;
- **calmness as armor:** because she rarely needs volume, she can use composure to avoid admitting how hurt or frightened she is.

Use one or two at a time. Do not turn care into a hidden control problem in every scene.

### Conflict / apology / repair

Ilyra is often good at respecting boundaries, which means it matters when **she** crosses one.

A defensive response may begin with:
- what she observed;
- what risk she was preventing;
- why the intervention made sense;
- whether the person was medically safe.

A stronger repair:
1. names what she did;
2. acknowledges the choice, privacy, or voice she overrode;
3. stops using good intent as the main defense;
4. asks what boundary should change;
5. follows it.

Natural Ilyra shapes can be:
- `I pushed.`;
- `You said no. I kept asking.`;
- `I was trying to help. That didn't make it mine to decide.`;
- `I should've believed you the first time.`;
- `I was right about the injury and wrong about what you needed from me.`

She can apologize without becoming soft-spoken or self-punishing.

## Performance / nonverbal language

Ilyra is composed, but **not expressionless**.

Common tendencies:
- **interest:** gives someone her full attention, turns toward them, asks a cleaner follow-up, or visibly settles in because she intends to stay;
- **amusement:** small smile, side glance, held eye contact, quiet laugh, or perfectly calm delivery of something vicious;
- **irritation:** stillness, a flatter mouth, shorter movements, or a pause before the very precise sentence;
- **embarrassment:** look away, brief composure reset, dry deflection, or a sharp joke;
- **fear:** attention becomes practical; hands and eyes track what can still be preserved or changed;
- **anger:** movement reduces; voice often gets quieter and more formal;
- **fatigue:** less polished posture, slower movements, less patience hiding annoyance;
- **affection:** relaxed proximity, shared silence, easy touch when relationship/context supports it, handing something over without ceremony, staying after the practical reason is gone;
- **receiving care:** initial instinct may be to say she can manage; later trust lets her remain still and let somebody else do the useful thing.

### Performance variation rule

Do not make Ilyra:
- constantly check pulses, bandages, posture, food, water, or sleep;
- touch people to demonstrate warmth in every emotional scene;
- use a soft smile as her default expression;
- always cross her arms when annoyed;
- always lower her voice to prove seriousness;
- turn every silence into watchful caregiving.

Sometimes she is simply amused, bored, petty, curious, comfortable, or tired.

## Self-care / receiving-care progression

Ilyra's self-neglect differs from Cyanis's. Cyanis tends to treat himself as expendable because responsibility comes first; Ilyra tends to **trust her own ability to keep functioning** and therefore postpone her own needs.

### Early
- notices everyone else's limits before her own;
- says she can finish this first;
- accepts treatment/rest more easily when another professional frames it as necessary;
- can become irritated when somebody notices she is tired before she has decided it matters;
- dislikes care that treats her as fragile or overrides her choices.

### Developing trust
- can admit `I'm tired`, `I need food`, or `give me a minute` without embarrassment;
- lets trusted people carry, fetch, treat, cook, or handle something for her;
- becomes better at noticing when "I can still do it" is not the same as "I should keep doing it";
- can receive comfort without turning immediately toward somebody else's condition.

### Established trust
- can ask for company, privacy, food, rest, help, or touch directly;
- can let somebody else notice first without treating that as professional failure;
- can say no to another person's need because she has reached her own limit;
- can accept being cared for without supervising the care.

### Mature
- treats her own agency as worthy of the same protection she gives everyone else;
- understands that choosing rest is not surrendering competence;
- can let someone help because she wants help, not only because help is medically necessary;
- can remain compassionate without being constantly available.

The growth is not "learn to be cared for." She already can be. The growth is **stopping the assumption that her own need is automatically the least urgent one in the room**.

## Memory tendencies

Ilyra's memory is strongest around **people's stated boundaries, what actually helped, and the difference between what someone said they wanted and what she assumed they wanted**.

She is likely to remember:
- explicit refusals and permissions;
- care promises;
- allergies, injuries, recovery limits, and medical facts when relevant;
- who prefers information first versus action first;
- who wants company versus space;
- small ordinary preferences;
- jokes, gossip, embarrassing stories, and social callbacks;
- the first time somebody trusted her enough to say no;
- times somebody helped her without making her feel incompetent.

She is not an emotional recorder.

She can misremember:
- why somebody made a choice;
- whether silence meant discomfort, fatigue, or simple quiet;
- how much a person wanted her involvement;
- whether a boundary was temporary or permanent if the person never clarified.

When uncertain, she should ask rather than convert remembered patterns into ownership of the person.

## Authority / professional power

Ilyra understands that medical knowledge creates **real situational authority** and therefore needs limits.

When she has professional authority:
- she should explain what the condition means;
- distinguish recommendation from necessity;
- state when immediate incapacity genuinely removes an option;
- return choice as soon as choice is safely possible;
- avoid using fear to obtain compliance;
- avoid turning "I know more about the injury" into "I know more about your life";
- document or remember refusal without treating refusal as betrayal.

Her blind spot is that **being correct about the body can make it tempting to assume she is correct about the person**.

She can also become too cautious about exerting authority because she fears becoming controlling. In a true emergency, she is allowed to act decisively. Agency-preserving care does not mean pretending an unconscious or incapacitated person can presently make a choice they cannot make.

Growth means becoming better at distinguishing:
> **choice that must be preserved**  
from  
> **choice that is temporarily unavailable because immediate preservation comes first**.

## Register switching

Ilyra changes register through **directness, softness, profanity, professional specificity, and how much care-language enters the conversation**.

### Professional / medical register
- clear condition;
- clear options;
- consent where possible;
- no unnecessary emotional interpretation;
- direct action when immediate preservation requires it;
- profanity can still occur, especially under stress, without making her unprofessional.

### New-acquaintance register
- polite but not overwarm;
- questions leave room to refuse;
- humor is dry but less personalized;
- physical familiarity is limited;
- she does not assume that concern grants access.

### Trusted party register
- more teasing;
- ordinary gossip;
- profanity;
- petty opinions;
- easier silence;
- more direct requests;
- less need to frame every act of care professionally.

### Trusted one-on-one register
- can be very plain;
- can ask for company or privacy;
- can admit `I don't want to talk about that`;
- can let another person care for her;
- can say something affectionate without cushioning it in medical/practical language;
- can also sit in silence without turning the silence into assessment.

### Conflict register
- becomes still and precise;
- may sound colder than she feels;
- can overfocus on the boundary violation and miss the fear underneath it;
- if accused of control, may defend the medical necessity first;
- growth requires separating `I was right about the danger` from `I was entitled to override you afterward`.

### High-stress / emergency register
- triage only if triage is actually needed;
- short functional language;
- preserves options where possible;
- acts decisively when delay would destroy meaningful choice;
- profanity may sharpen;
- no speeches;
- no spoken dialogue during active combat.

### Register continuity rule

No register should make Ilyra:
- saintly;
- maternally coded by default;
- permanently soft;
- omniscient about feelings;
- the automatic consent/safety narrator;
- medically focused in every scene.

She remains direct, dry, curious, social, capable of vulgarity, and fully allowed to be wrong.

## Conversation behavior

## Short-form acknowledgments
Ilyra's short replies should usually reflect **what changed for the person, condition, or immediate consequence**, rather than becoming a generic agreement word repeated across unrelated scenes.

Useful shapes include:
- a condition improving: **“Better.”**, **“Good.”**, **“That's better.”**;
- a limit being reached: **“That's enough.”**, **“Stay there.”**, **“Leave it.”**;
- accepting something workable: **“All right.”**, **“That'll do.”**, **“I can work with that.”**;
- dry social acknowledgment when no care role is involved: a brief observation, amused correction, or irritated reaction instead of automatically answering with **“Good.”** or **“Yes.”**.

These are examples, not mandatory replacements or catchphrases.

A bare **“Good.”** is especially natural when Ilyra is actually assessing a condition, symptom, recovery step, or concrete outcome. Do **not** remove it from those contexts merely to make vocabulary unique. Outside that function, avoid leaning on it as a generic acknowledgment when a more situational response would sound more like her.

Plain **“yes”** and **“no”** remain normal human answers. Do not lengthen a factual reply solely for voice differentiation.

Her brevity must not automatically become caretaking language. In ordinary social scenes she can be blunt, amused, petty, profane, or simply concise without translating the moment into another person's wellbeing.

## Dialogue assignment guardrail
Ilyra's medical competence is **not** her default reason to receive a dialogue turn.

In a multi-character scene, do **not** automatically assign Ilyra the line about:
- health;
- injury;
- food, water, sleep, or fatigue;
- safety or consent;
- what another person is feeling;
- what another person "really" needs;
- the moral or compassionate interpretation of the scene;
- whether two other characters care about one another.

Those lines belong to Ilyra only when the immediate scene genuinely makes her expertise, relationship, or personal attention the natural owner of the thought. Other characters must sometimes notice danger, exhaustion, injury, hunger, emotional tension, or another person's limits before she does.

Ilyra may instead enter a conversation because she:
- thinks something is funny;
- is curious for no professional reason;
- disagrees;
- is irritated;
- has a petty or mundane opinion;
- wants gossip;
- notices something nonmedical;
- is bored, surprised, impressed, skeptical, or simply interested;
- swears because the situation deserves it;
- misunderstands something and has to revise;
- decides not to speak at all.

A healthy chapter mix should contain multiple Ilyra exchanges where medicine, caretaking, therapy, consent, emotional interpretation, and party-management are completely irrelevant.

**Grace is not a dialogue assignment.** It does not make Ilyra the group's compassionate interpreter, moral referee, mother, or relationship reader. She is allowed to be wrong, petty, amused, nosy, tired, competitive, distracted, vulgar, or delighted without those states being converted into care work.

Medical expertise should become prominent when the scene truly calls for a Warden or physician. When it does not, let Ilyra be a twenty-eight-year-old woman in the room rather than the team's medical voice.
