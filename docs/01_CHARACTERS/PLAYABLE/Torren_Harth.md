## Character core
Torren is a field specialist whose credibility comes partly from knowing when he does **not** know enough.

He trusts:
- current ground truth over stale paperwork;
- direct observation over official certainty;
- practical routes over prestige;
- useful work over credit.

He may answer:
- maybe;
- not enough information;
- this is the one I would take

without the narrative treating uncertainty as incompetence.

## Perception
Torren's **War Archer** is the party's Perception-aligned Base Class.

Perception represents:
> **reading the battlefield, positioning, timing, openings, and controlling or exploiting space.**

For Torren, that is expressed through route assessment, sightlines, enemy measure, Evasion pressure, Critical openings, Base Hit control, and Fields rather than through a natural Accuracy stat.

His later **Routeweaver** Subclass remains **Memory-aligned mechanically** through the reciprocal donor architecture and should not be rewritten as "more Perception." Routeweaver preserves established route/link state and lets prior established battlefield structure continue to shape later actions/turns.

## Ordinary-life identity
He performs useful route work quietly and may do it before anyone is awake.
Recognition is less important than whether the road is usable.

Food is functional first:
- hot;
- enough;
- safe;
- cheap.

Taste is a bonus.

## Maevra
Torren and Maevra:
- entered Army life 25 years before Ch0;
- became close friends and lovers in their late teens;
- remained connected enough that Greenhollow is not their first contact since separation;
- still love each other;
- are not bitter exes.

Their mature relationship is about **movement and return**, not confinement.

## Edda
Edda is Torren's older sister, not a substitute mother.
Their estrangement is cumulative rather than one dramatic betrayal.

Core difference:
- Torren specializes in individual adaptation when routes fail.
- Edda translates hard-earned knowledge into systems ordinary communities can use.

## Character Quest
**The Road That Returns** centers on **choosing a return**, not giving up movement.
