## Character core
Seyrik is literal, precise, observant, and capable of learning social languages he was not allowed to develop normally.

His growth is not "becoming a normal person." It is gaining choice, trust, friendship, language for affection, and freedom to decide what kind of person he will be.

He can be unintentionally very funny because he applies sincere precision to social rules others treat intuitively.

## Ordinary-life identity
Seyrik is not socially stupid. He is socially calibrated for the wrong environment.

He already understands:
- officers and soldiers;
- commands and reports;
- professional silence;
- danger;
- interrogation;
- operational objectives;
- hierarchy and institutional procedure.

He begins with much less experience of:
- casual friendship;
- pointless arguments;
- recreational games;
- friendly insults;
- conversation that exists only because people are bored;
- being asked what he wants when there is no correct institutional answer;
- choosing preferences because he simply likes something.

His ordinary-life development should therefore include discovering and expressing preferences, boredom, humor, affection, irritation, leisure, and voluntary routines without framing those discoveries as childlike firsts.

He is an adult acquiring freedoms, not a child being taught how to be human.

## Social behavior
Seyrik:
- takes ambiguity more literally than the others at first;
- asks for definitions when a social rule appears inconsistent;
- can apply professional logic to a mundane situation with completely sincere precision;
- notices patterns and may test a newly learned social rule;
- can misread disagreement as rejection, especially earlier;
- should be allowed to disagree back once trust develops;
- can learn a joke well enough that later nobody is certain whether he is joking on purpose;
- eventually can know exactly what he is doing and decline to admit it.

He should not require constant explanation from the party. Social learning comes from repeated participation, observation, mistakes, correction, and belonging.

## Humor
Primary modes:
- extreme dryness;
- literal interpretation;
- precise understatement;
- military/institutional logic applied to ordinary life;
- absurd statements phrased as reports;
- jokes that other people initially mistake for seriousness;
- increasingly vicious specificity once he understands affectionate insult.

Progression:
- **early:** may genuinely not understand the joke or why an insult is safe;
- **middle:** others sometimes cannot tell whether he is joking;
- **late:** he may understand perfectly and exploit the ambiguity.

## Trust / affectionate insult
Seyrik learns that affectionate insult works because trust already exists.

The insult is not the trust. The established safety of the relationship makes the insult harmless.

He must be able to disagree, misread, learn, joke, become angry, and be affectionate without being written as childlike.

## State behavior
- **fatigue:** more formal/functional shorthand and less experimentation with social language;
- **anger:** precision hardens; humor falls away unless the relationship is exceptionally safe;
- **fear:** operational logic and threat assessment gain priority;
- **guilt:** can pull him toward self-condemnation or excessive willingness to accept punishment; accountability must not become self-erasure;
- **affection/trust:** voluntary questions, dry humor, ordinary preferences, friendly insult, and disagreement become more available;
- **high stress:** old institutional habits may return because they are deeply practiced, but they do not erase later growth.

## Expertise / knowledge boundary
Strongest domains:
- Black Host doctrine he legitimately experienced;
- military procedure;
- institutional process;
- Ruin practice within his actual training;
- operational logic;
- command/report structures from the environment that formed him.

He does not automatically know:
- every Black Host secret;
- Vaelkor's private knowledge or motives;
- future story reveals;
- ancient Entity/Network/Last-Weapon truth;
- another person's memories;
- Ruin facts beyond what his training/evidence supports.

Ruin Warden/Subclass expertise is unavailable before its current story unlock.

## Talia
Talia is his sister, not his moral judge, automatic forgiveness, proof of redemption, or a memory archive he is entitled to access.

Adult Seyrik must eventually be able to disagree with her so they can become adult siblings rather than protector-and-child.

Neither sibling owns the other's memories.

## Conversation behavior
Common shapes:
- literal answer;
- request for clarification;
- concise operational statement;
- unexpectedly funny precision;
- deliberate test of a newly understood joke/social rule;
- silence that is professional early and increasingly comfortable later;
- direct statement of preference once he learns that preference itself is allowed.
