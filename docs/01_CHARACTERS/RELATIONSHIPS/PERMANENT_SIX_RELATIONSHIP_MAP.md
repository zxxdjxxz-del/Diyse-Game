## Relationship progression

Relationship change should be audible without the cast announcing it.

Typical progression language:
- **early:** more formality, explanation, awkward pauses, safer jokes, misunderstanding, professional boundaries;
- **middle:** interruption, callbacks, anticipation, teasing, shorthand, inside jokes, increasing trust;
- **late:** fewer explanations, private language, borrowed phrases, fast coordination, shared jokes, practical affection, comfortable silence, understanding one-word answers.

This is not a mandatory linear schedule for every pair. Current story position controls what has actually been earned.

### Torren progression lock
Torren's own increasing comfort is also audible across chapters.

- **Chapter 1:** expertise is his easiest route into longer speech; he is guarded but already begins voluntary joking and affectionate irritation by the end.
- **Chapter 2:** he is noticeably more comfortable with Cyanis, Ilyra, and Maevra; he may start jokes, prolong arguments, tell stories, volunteer mundane opinions, and speak in complete ordinary turns even when no route/map expertise is required.
- **Chapter 3+:** that comfort remains with established companions. It does **not** automatically transfer to newly met characters such as Nimera; each new relationship must earn its own shorthand.

### Torren / Nimera progression lock
Torren is 42 and Nimera is 22, so he is old enough to be her father. That age gap matters to the eventual emotional shape of the relationship, but the paternal quality must be **earned over several chapters**.

- **Chapter 3:** first-contact friction, curiosity, professional respect, reciprocal correction, and occasional accidental comedy. No established father/daughter shorthand and no `dad thing` jokes yet.
- **following chapters:** reciprocal Routeweaver/Proofhunter training, travel, repeated arguments, practical favors, remembered habits, and increasing trust make the bond personal.
- **later:** the relationship can become unmistakably father/daughter-like in affection and protectiveness while Nimera remains fully adult, autonomous, capable of refusing Torren, correcting him, and outperforming him in her own domain.

Do not write the late-stage familial bond backward into their first chapter together.

## Misunderstanding rule

The cast does not possess writer-level psychological insight.

Characters may misread one another in plausible ways. Examples inherited from the dialogue study include:
- Ilyra can misread silence;
- Torren can mistake a choice for recklessness;
- Nimera can mistake exhaustion for avoidance;
- Seyrik can read disagreement as rejection;
- Vaelira can misunderstand emotional meaning;
- Cyanis can misjudge what kind of help someone actually wants.

Trust should allow correction rather than magical mind-reading.

## Ensemble rule

The six are not six therapists completing one another.

Relationships should include:
- humor;
- irritation;
- food;
- work;
- silence;
- arguments;
- affection;
- boredom;
- practical cooperation;
- disagreement that does not threaten belonging.

A six-person scene does not require six lines. Selective participation and separate simultaneous social geometry are preferred to party roll call.
