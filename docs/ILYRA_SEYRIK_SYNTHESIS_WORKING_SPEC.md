## 1. Pair thesis

Ilyra and Seyrik integrate the same relationship from opposite directions.

- **Ilyra:** preservation gives her permission to press the attack, and successful Vowblade mercy makes her next return to Blue Warden discipline more efficient.
- **Seyrik:** Ruin Vanguard offense creates the opening for preservation, and successful Ruin Warden preservation feeds disciplined force back into Ruin Vanguard.

The pair therefore creates two opposite combat cadences without simply granting either character more raw healing.

Neither Synthesis creates a new gauge, extra action, extra Trait, new weapon requirement, or new healing/status subsystem.

---

## 2. Ilyra Synthesis — Mercy Unbroken

**Mercy Unbroken** is Ilyra's permanent Synthesis passive after the universal Synthesis gate is met and the node is purchased.

### A. Blue Warden → Vowblade bridge

**Once per round**, after Ilyra resolves a **non-Ultimate Blue Warden Ability** that either:
- restores HP to another ally; or
- removes at least one ordinary harmful status from another ally,

her next **damaging non-Ultimate Vowblade Ability** before the end of the following round gains **+10% Power**.

Boundaries:
- self-only recovery does not qualify;
- one qualifying Blue Warden action arms one one-use bonus;
- the bonus does not stack with itself;
- it does not alter the Vowblade Ability's Hybrid formula, targeting, hit count, penetration, MP cost, or weapon legality;
- Blue Warden Ultimates do not arm this clause.

### B. Vowblade → Blue Warden bridge

**Once per round**, when a **Vowblade Ability or Ultimate** restores HP to at least one conscious ally other than Ilyra, her next **non-Ultimate Blue Warden Ability** before the end of the following round costs **3 less MP**, minimum 1 MP.

Boundaries:
- an action that heals multiple allies still arms only one discount;
- the discount does not stack with itself;
- the discount applies to the next legal non-Ultimate Blue Warden Ability only;
- the discount does not alter healing Power, status removal, revival value, target rules, or timing;
- Mercy in Steel's self-healing alone does not qualify because another ally must actually receive HP recovery.

### C. Intent

Ilyra's late-game integration loop becomes:

**preserve someone → press the attack → turn offensive mercy back into efficient preservation.**

This lets Blue Warden and Vowblade actively feed one another without turning Vowblade into a second pure-healing kit or allowing Blue Warden to inherit Ruin Vanguard's raw offense directly.

## 3. Seyrik Synthesis — Tempered Ruin

**Tempered Ruin** is Seyrik's permanent Synthesis passive after the universal Synthesis gate is met and the node is purchased.

### A. Ruin Vanguard → Ruin Warden bridge

**Once per round**, after Seyrik resolves a **damaging non-Ultimate Ruin Vanguard Ability**, his next **non-Ultimate Ruin Warden Ability** before the end of the following round costs **3 less MP**, minimum 1 MP.

Boundaries:
- one qualifying Ruin Vanguard action arms one discount;
- the discount does not stack with itself;
- it does not alter the Ruin Warden Ability's Power, healing, Drain, status removal, revival value, targeting, or weapon legality;
- Ruin Vanguard Ultimates do not arm this clause.

### B. Ruin Warden → Ruin Vanguard bridge

**Once per round**, when a **Ruin Warden Ability or Ultimate** restores HP to at least one ally other than Seyrik, his next **damaging non-Ultimate Ruin Vanguard Ability** before the end of the following round gains **+10% Power**.

Boundaries:
- an action that restores multiple allies still arms only one one-use bonus;
- the Power bonus does not stack with itself;
- Siphon Rune's self-recovery alone does not qualify;
- Stolen Grace, Restoring Ward, Withering Mercy, Reclaimed Breath, and Mercy Through Ruin may qualify when they actually restore HP to another ally;
- revival through Reclaimed Breath counts because the revived ally returns with restored HP;
- the bonus does not change damage type, hit count, self-drawback clauses, cooldowns, MP cost, target rules, or weapon legality.

### C. Intent

Seyrik's late-game integration loop becomes:

**use Ruin decisively → make preservation easier → let successful preservation discipline the next use of Ruin.**

He does not become gentler by ceasing to be a Ruin Vanguard. He becomes more controlled because Ilyra's Warden discipline gives him a deliberate way to move between destruction and preservation.

## 4. Pair balance boundaries

- Neither Synthesis grants flat permanent stats.
- Neither adds raw healing percentages or new revival rules.
- Neither grants extra ordinary actions, extra Card actions, extra equipment slots, extra Card slots, or a second Trait.
- The +10% Power bonuses apply only to the next qualifying **non-Ultimate** damaging Ability.
- The 3-MP discounts apply only to the next qualifying **non-Ultimate** Ability and cannot reduce cost below 1 MP.
- A qualifying trigger may arm only one copy of its effect; repeated triggers do not stack multiple future bonuses/discounts.
- No Ability or Ultimate gains a weapon requirement.
- No additional pair bonus is granted merely for fielding Ilyra and Seyrik together.

The effects are intentionally cadence-oriented rather than raw-stat-oriented because Synthesis already also grants paired Legacy eligibility.

## 5. Approved resolution / integration story beat — Keep Them Alive

### Placement

- **Late Chapter 11**, at the **Forward Hub** in captured Black Host territory, after an already-authored field operation and before Chapter 12's point of no return.
- Use the Forward Hub's existing treatment / recovery staging rather than inventing a bespoke location.
- Exact final scene number remains for later script integration.
- The scene is **mandatory authored continuity**, not a missable camp event, romance scene, or dialogue-choice reward.
- Completing it satisfies the **character-resolution / integration prerequisite for both Ilyra and Seyrik**. It does not automatically buy Synthesis; each still needs the full mechanical gate and 1 MP.

### Setup

An **unnamed wounded Black Host captive** from the current Chapter 11 campaign has been taken alive. The captive is disarmed, restrained, and in immediate need of treatment. The character is not introduced as a new recurring NPC and receives no redemption arc inside this beat.

Ilyra begins treating the captive. Seyrik is present because his knowledge of Black Host armor, training, and prisoner behavior is useful. Crucially, the scene does **not** manufacture a false moral argument where Ilyra wants the captive saved and Seyrik wants them dead.

Seyrik never argues that treatment should be withheld. Ilyra never argues that keeping someone alive means forgiving them.

Their disagreement is about **what mercy is allowed to require**.

### The practical problem

Part of the captive's damaged armor is pinning or obstructing access to the wound. It cannot simply be pulled free without making the injury worse.

Seyrik can sever the damaged section cleanly with controlled Ruin technique, but the cut has to be decisive. Ilyra can stabilize and protect the person through the removal, but she cannot make the dangerous step disappear merely because she would prefer a gentler option.

Ilyra hesitates for a moment—not because she is afraid to act, but because the immediate correct action deliberately risks causing more pain and damage in order to prevent something worse.

Seyrik recognizes the hesitation. His point is practical: there is no clean version of this problem. Waiting for one is still a choice, and in this case it is the worse one.

### First integration — force inside a boundary

Ilyra establishes exactly what must be protected and tells Seyrik where he can cut.

- Her Warden discipline defines the boundary.
- Seyrik applies Ruin only inside the necessary line.
- The damaged armor comes free.
- Ilyra immediately stabilizes the injury.

The visual language should be restrained: blue-white Warden geometry establishes the safe boundary; controlled violet Ruin severs only what has to be removed.

This is not presented as Ruin becoming harmless. It is presented as **force given a purpose and an endpoint**.

### Second integration — preservation chooses the hard action

A second intervention is required. This time Ilyra does not wait for Seyrik to tell her what the harsher option is.

She identifies what has to happen and gives the instruction herself.

The point is not that Seyrik teaches Ilyra to be cruel. The point is that Ilyra accepts that preserving life can sometimes require a deliberately forceful act, and refusing to make that choice does not automatically make the refusal merciful.

Seyrik, in turn, follows her limit exactly. He does not take the opportunity to do more damage simply because doing damage is easier for him.

That restraint matters as much as Ilyra's decision.

### After the treatment

The captive survives and remains restrained.

The conversation that follows should make three distinctions explicit in intent, though exact lines remain deferred:

1. **Mercy is not forgiveness.** Keeping someone alive does not erase what they have done or exempt them from consequence.
2. **Restraint is not passivity.** A boundary may have to be enforced with force to mean anything.
3. **Violence is not automatically decisiveness.** Seyrik's growth is shown by stopping exactly where the necessary action ends.

Seyrik's history with the Black Host informs the scene without turning into an exposition dump. He knows better than most that a Black Host uniform does not tell the complete story of how someone arrived inside it, but the scene does not generalize every Black Host soldier into an unwilling victim or offer blanket absolution.

### Character resolution

**Ilyra learns:** mercy cannot be defined only by how little pain she personally chooses to cause. Sometimes protecting someone requires a hard act, and moral responsibility includes owning that act rather than pretending inaction is cleaner.

This completes the emotional logic behind **Vowblade** and **Mercy Unbroken**: her willingness to fight can be an extension of preservation rather than a betrayal of it.

**Seyrik learns:** control is not merely reducing the amount of destruction. It is knowing exactly what the destruction is for and stopping when that purpose has been achieved.

This completes the emotional logic behind **Ruin Warden** and **Tempered Ruin**: preservation does not weaken Ruin; it gives Ruin a boundary that Seyrik himself chooses to honor.

Neither character adopts the other's morality wholesale. Ilyra does not become comfortable with unnecessary violence. Seyrik does not become sentimental about enemies or consequences.

### Tone / dialogue boundary

- Keep the scene adult, practical, and unsentimental.
- Avoid a speech about forgiveness, redemption, or the healing power of friendship.
- Ilyra should not patronize Seyrik about his Black Host history.
- Seyrik should not mock Ilyra's mercy as naïve; by this point he understands that her discipline is difficult precisely because it requires choices.
- Dry humor is allowed around the work itself, especially once the immediate danger has passed.
- Exact final dialogue remains deferred to the later dialogue/script pass.

Possible dialogue intent, **not locked final wording**:
- Ilyra makes clear that treatment is not pardon.
- Seyrik answers that he was not asking for pardon.
- One of them states, in plain language, that the point is to **keep them alive long enough to answer for themselves**.

### Closing image

The captive is alive, bandaged, and still securely restrained.

Ilyra checks the dressing once more. Seyrik checks the restraint—not roughly, simply correctly. Before he leaves, he places water where the captive will be able to reach it when awake without compromising the restraint.

Ilyra notices. She says nothing.

Seyrik does not explain it.

The scene ends there.

### Production / implementation boundaries

- Reuse the existing Forward Hub treatment/recovery environment family and ordinary Black Host soldier assets.
- The captive remains unnamed and does not require a bespoke portrait, persistent NPC record, quest, or later redemption content.
- Reuse Ilyra's blue-white Warden VFX and Seyrik's restrained violet Ruin VFX; do not use Black Host corruption effects for Seyrik's learned Warden expression.
- No combat encounter, boss, item reward, new lore artifact, or new system is introduced.
- The only system result is marking the **Ilyra Synthesis story prerequisite complete** and the **Seyrik Synthesis story prerequisite complete**.
- If the remaining mechanical gates are incomplete, the Mastery interface may show the narrative prerequisite as completed while the Synthesis node remains unavailable.
