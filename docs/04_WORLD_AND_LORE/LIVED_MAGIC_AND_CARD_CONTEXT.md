# DIYSE — Lived Magic & Card Context

**Status:** ACTIVE WORLD-LIFE / KNOWLEDGE AUTHORITY  
**Domain:** `04_WORLD_AND_LORE`  
**Purpose:** define the magical reality modern people actually grow up inside so character agents distinguish ordinary magic and Standard Cards from the almost-mythic Prime category.

This file governs **character/world knowledge and lived assumptions**, not combat numbers. Exact class mechanics remain in `06_CLASSES_AND_ABILITIES`; exact Card/Prime mechanics remain in `07_CARDS`; story reveal timing remains in `02_STORY`.

## Natural magic is ordinary life

Abilities are natural forms of magic expressed through the person using them.

A trained Ability may depend on:
- class/professional training;
- the user's own magical capability;
- physical technique;
- weapon practice;
- learned magical control;
- instinct and repeated experience.

Modern people do not experience an ordinary Ability as a videogame command or as an inexplicable supernatural event.

The mere fact that somebody used magic is normally unremarkable. What may be remarkable is:
- unusual strength;
- difficult technique;
- precision;
- rarity;
- dangerous application;
- a school or effect the observer has little experience with;
- somebody doing something they should not plausibly know how to do.

A character should therefore react to **what was done and how well**, not mechanically announce that magic occurred.

## Standard Cards are normal modern objects

Standard Cards are surviving Ancient Diysean magical artifacts.

Their basic function is common modern knowledge:

> **A Standard Card contains/preserves an Ancient Diysean ability and grants the person holding/using that Card access to that ability.**

Cards are not restricted to chosen bloodlines, prophecy, or one special modern institution.

Any ordinary person can in principle obtain, possess, and use a Standard Card if they actually acquire one. Actual availability, rarity, ownership, reward placement, and commerce remain governed by their owning systems; this statement does not create ordinary shop sales or random drops.

Modern people have centuries of practical Card experience. They may:
- recognize familiar Card types or effects;
- own or have owned Cards;
- inherit them;
- recover them;
- receive them;
- trade or transfer them where setting/economy authority permits;
- build professional or combat practice around them;
- have opinions about useful, rare, awkward, overrated, dangerous, or desirable Cards.

Therefore:
- "this is a Card" is not itself a mystery;
- Ancient origin does not make a Standard Card socially exotic;
- a character does not explain basic Card function to another informed adult without a real reason;
- a Standard Card activation is not automatically a major story event;
- unusual Card behavior is judged against a strong shared expectation of how normal Cards behave.

## Why Cyanis's recovered Card is strange

The recovered Card matters early because a familiar category begins violating familiar rules.

Modern characters already know what ordinary Cards normally do.

Therefore unusual evidence includes behavior such as:
- an effect occurring without a normal deliberate Card use;
- the Card appearing to track or respond to one person's condition;
- effects that do not match a known ordinary Card ability;
- persistent state changes without an ordinary explanation;
- behavior that trained Card specialists cannot classify from normal practice.

Those observations justify uncertainty.

They do **not** justify immediately calling the Card a Prime.

## Prime Cards — modern epistemic firewall

Prime Cards are fundamentally different from Standard Cards in modern knowledge.

Hard modern-history locks:

> **No one in known modern history has knowingly possessed a Prime Card.**

> **No one in known modern history has knowingly activated or used a Prime Card.**

The term **Prime** survives only in a very small number of surviving late-Diysean records.

Those records are sparse enough that:
- most people are uncertain whether Primes were real, mythic, ceremonial, exaggerated, mistranslated, or simply poorly understood;
- scholars may disagree about how literally the records should be read;
- even specialists do not possess a verified modern example against which to test the texts.

The one broad surviving implication is:

> **If Prime Cards were real, they were understood to be the most powerful / highest-order Cards.**

Modern people do **not** begin the story knowing:
- what a Prime actually does;
- how one activates;
- what manifestation means;
- Recovered/Awakened progression;
- Prime combat rounds or commands;
- Prime slots/loadout rules;
- restoration/use limits;
- Story-Prime bearer relationships;
- Major-Hunt Prime structure;
- the six sacrifice-warrior truth;
- any player-facing or repository-facing Prime mechanics.

Those facts are author/game-system truth until the story legitimately reveals them.

## Character knowledge calibration

### Ordinary modern person
Can plausibly know:
- magic is normal;
- trained people have Abilities;
- Cards are Ancient Diysean artifacts;
- a Standard Card grants access to its preserved ability;
- Cards differ in usefulness/rarity;
- some old stories or scholarly references may mention exceptionally powerful Cards.

Should not automatically know:
- the technical term Prime;
- whether Primes were real;
- any Prime mechanics.

### Educated / historically literate person
May plausibly have encountered the word **Prime** in secondary scholarship or rare-record discussion.

Their knowledge ceiling remains:
- sparse late-Diysean references;
- uncertain historicity;
- reputation as extraordinarily powerful if genuine;
- no verified modern holder/use;
- no reliable description of function.

### Card specialist / archive specialist
May know more about:
- provenance;
- variant readings;
- which surviving records use the term;
- why scholars disagree;
- known Standard Card behavior.

They still do **not** know Prime operation without story-earned evidence.

Expertise permits better uncertainty, not omniscience.

## Dialogue / Agent-Brain rule

Every character agent should implicitly compare events against this baseline:

1. Is this ordinary personal magic? Treat it as ordinary unless execution is exceptional.
2. Is this a normal Standard Card doing a known Card thing? Do not manufacture mystery.
3. Is a Standard Card behaving outside established modern expectations? Notice the violation specifically.
4. Does the evidence actually justify invoking the disputed Prime category?
5. If Prime is discussed, is the speaker expressing verified knowledge, scholarship, rumor, inference, or belief?
6. Never import player-facing Prime mechanics into character cognition.

The dramatic power of a Prime reveal depends on modern people already being comfortable with normal magic and normal Cards.
