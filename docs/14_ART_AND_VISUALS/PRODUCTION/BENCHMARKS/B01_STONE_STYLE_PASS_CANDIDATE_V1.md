## Approved B01 stone read

Diyse stone uses:

- large masonry masses that read before small cracks;
- broad painterly planes rather than flat fills or photo-noise;
- deliberately irregular block shapes without random rubble everywhere;
- strongest line weight at silhouette, deep joints, damage, and contact shadow;
- thinner, broken, tapered marks for interior fractures;
- linework that falls away on light-facing planes;
- moss entering from joints, moisture pockets, rubble, and ground contact;
- increased line density on damaged stone compared with clean stone;
- one shared material grammar across walls, arches, pillars, corners, ledges, stairs, rubble, and floors;
- a neutral material base that remains usable under warm sun, cool sky, overcast, torch, magic, and night lighting.

## Approved line-density target

**Too clean:**
- uniform smooth edges;
- barely visible line variation;
- flat digital-paint look;
- sterile masonry.

**Too heavy:**
- every block fully outlined;
- deep-black mortar grid everywhere;
- comic-book contour dominance;
- excessive crack density;
- loss of structural read at field scale.

**Diyse target:**
- bold irregular silhouette accents;
- broken interior strokes;
- selective black clusters in occlusion/damage;
- broad painterly stone faces;
- quiet light-facing areas;
- controlled hand-drawn asymmetry.

## Approved material family

The baseline Diyse-original stone family includes:

- neutral cut stone;
- weathered stone;
- damaged/cracked stone;
- mossy stone;
- wet/damp stone;
- ancient carved stone;
- etched/ritual stone;
- floor tiles;
- straight walls;
- inner/outer corners;
- caps and ledges;
- arches and door surrounds;
- pillars/abutments;
- stairs/ramps;
- rubble/breakage clusters.

Regional and faction variants may change palette, construction rhythm, ornament, damage, vegetation, and accent language while preserving the baseline B01 rendering grammar.
