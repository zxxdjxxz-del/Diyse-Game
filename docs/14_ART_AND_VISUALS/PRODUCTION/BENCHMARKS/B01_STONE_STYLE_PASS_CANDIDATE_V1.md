## Candidate read

The candidate establishes a useful visual target for Diyse stone:

- large masonry masses read before small cracks;
- stone surfaces use broad painterly planes rather than flat fills or photo-noise;
- block shapes are irregular without becoming random rubble everywhere;
- line weight is strongest at silhouette, deep joints, damage, and contact shadow;
- interior fractures use thinner, broken, tapered marks;
- linework falls away on light-facing planes;
- moss enters from joints, moisture pockets, rubble, and ground contact rather than being pasted uniformly across every stone;
- damaged stone carries more line density than clean stone;
- curved walls, arches, pillars, corners, ledges, stairs, rubble, and floor tiles can share one material grammar;
- warm sun, cool sky, overcast, torch, magic, and night lighting can all sit on the same neutral base material;
- the look remains readable in an HD-2D exploration mockup.

## Refinement pressure before acceptance

The target should preserve the roughness and ink energy without drifting into either extreme:

**Too clean:**
- uniform smooth edges;
- barely visible line variation;
- flat digital-paint look;
- sterile masonry.

**Too heavy:**
- every block fully outlined;
- deep-black mortar grid everywhere;
- comic-book contour dominance;
- excessive crack density;
- loss of structural read at field scale.

**Target:**
- bold irregular silhouette accents;
- broken interior strokes;
- selective black clusters in occlusion/damage;
- broad painterly stone faces;
- quiet light-facing areas;
- hand-drawn asymmetry that remains controlled.

## Proposed B01 material family if approved

The candidate supports a reusable Diyse-original stone family containing:

- neutral cut stone;
- weathered stone;
- damaged/cracked stone;
- mossy stone;
- wet/damp stone;

- etched/ritual stone;
- floor tiles;
- straight walls;
- inner/outer corners;

- arches and door surrounds;
- pillars/abutments;
- stairs/ramps;
- rubble/breakage clusters.

Regional and faction variants should alter palette, construction rhythm, ornament, damage, vegetation, and accent language without replacing this baseline stone rendering grammar.
