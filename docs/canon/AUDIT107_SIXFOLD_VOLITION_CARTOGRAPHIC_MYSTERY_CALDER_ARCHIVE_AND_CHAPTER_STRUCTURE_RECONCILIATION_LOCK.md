## 1. Sixfold Volition — story placement and meaning

The formal name remains **The Sixfold Volition**. `Sixfold Accord` is deprecated.

### 1.1 Placement

Locked story placement:
- **Chapter 6 ends with Seyrik's conditional permanent recruitment.**
- Chapter 6 does **not** immediately proceed into the Sixfold Volition or universal Subclass training.
- **Chapter 7 — The Prison of Names** is the first full mandatory chapter in which all six permanent party members must function together as a roster.
- The **Sixfold Volition occurs at the end of Chapter 7 / the Cresthaven return after Chapter 7**.
- **Chapter 8** is the first full mandatory chapter designed around sustained use of the post-Volition Subclass architecture.

This placement supersedes older structures that used the full-roster milestone itself as sufficient justification for the Subclass unlock.

### 1.2 Meaning

The Sixfold Volition is fundamentally an act of **six independent wills choosing**.

It represents the six permanent party members freely deciding to entrust their paths/traditions to one another and begin cross-training. The event must preserve personal agency:
- no magical mind-link forces agreement;
- no prophecy or destiny compels the choice;
- no Ancient mechanism chooses on their behalf;
- each person retains a distinct will and identity;
- the shared result exists because all six choose participation.

Mechanically, Audit104 remains controlling:
- no permanent character has or uses a Subclass before the Volition;
- all six Subclasses unlock at the Volition;
- reciprocal cross-training begins afterward through the locked Audit104 pairs.

The later scene rewrite must make the event feel earned by Chapter 7's full-roster experience rather than functioning as a roster-completion tutorial.

## 3. Long-form Ancient cartographic mystery — controlling progression

The map mystery is **not** a set of six magical jigsaw pieces and is **not** tied numerically to the six Faces or six permanent characters.

The party accumulates multiple physical cartographic sources made for different Ancient purposes. These sources use related positional / relational grammar, and their meaning grows only through comparison over many chapters.

Current controlling sequence:

1. **Chapter 1 — Hollow Watch rubbing**  
   A local route source showing where one route continued.

2. **Chapter 1 — Wayfinder material / surviving copies**  
   A broader Ancient cartographic source showing how a larger set of routes relate to terrain and one another.

3. **Chapter 3 — post-Warden routing sketch**  
   Torren's practical copy from the command-record room's second routing display; it describes roads, high ground, water, destination geometry, and repeats the same broader relational language. It leads the party to Cresthaven under existing Chapter 3 canon.

4. **Chapter 4 — Vaelira / Reaction Annex related transcription or chart**  
   Vaelira recognizes that the earlier map materials use a positional structure related to something she has seen in her own work. This source is not required to be a literal travel map; it is a modern transcription/chart using compatible spatial/relational grammar. Current terminology is **Reaction Annex**; `Sixfold Annex` is deprecated for this non-party location/material context.

5. **Chapter 5 — Deepforge works survey / related physical map**  
   Found naturally in the aftermath of the **Deepforge Colossus / deeper sovereign-maintenance sequence**. It belongs to Deepforge's construction, maintenance, transport, survey, or works history rather than existing as an arbitrary lore collectible.

6. **Chapter 8 — Westreach related map / survey**  
   A western survey source that overlaps the older Wayfinder-area material and expands the party's reliable picture of Ancient Yahtrea.

7. **Chapter 10 — final missing eastern survey / map source**  
   Recovered in the new mandatory eastern chapter, presently planned around a second/eastern Wayfinder-type site. It fills the eastern blind spot and lets the party reconcile the physical picture of Ancient Yahtrea before Crown Engine.

The exact final forms/titles of the Chapter 5, Chapter 8, and Chapter 10 physical documents may be refined in scene production without changing their story functions.

## 4. Chapter 4 → Chapter 5 delayed map correction

At the end of Chapter 4, the party is allowed to believe that its interpretation of the earlier maps may have been partly wrong because the evidence appeared to lead to **Ivorybridge** without revealing an obvious larger destination.

Chapter 5 corrects that conclusion.

After the Deepforge Colossus, the Deepforge survey proves:
- the earlier route did **not** terminate at Ivorybridge;
- Ivorybridge lay **along** the route;
- the route continued farther north into the Greyspires toward **Deepforge**;
- the party's earlier cartographic reasoning was incomplete rather than wholly false.

This is a direct thematic payoff to Vaelira's Chapter 4 boundary lesson: the party mistook the edge of the information it possessed for the edge of the thing itself.

The route also happened to bring the party through Ivorybridge, where it encountered Vaelira. This is **not prophecy or destiny**. The road physically passed through the place where they met her.

The Deepforge survey must **not** point directly toward Westreach as the next destination. Deepforge is far north; Westreach is geographically close to the Wayfinder-area western material. Chapter 5 proves northward continuity and shared cartographic language, not a direct Deepforge→Westreach route instruction.

## 6. New Chapter 10 — eastern blind spot and second Wayfinder

Chapter 10 is a mandatory connective/investigative chapter placed **after Crownfall/Rhazek and before Crown Engine**.

Its purpose is to tie together long-running mysteries rather than introduce another unrelated giant war siege.

### 6.1 Core objective

The accumulated maps leave eastern Yahtrea inadequately explained. The party deliberately seeks the missing eastern cartographic evidence.

The current approved dungeon direction is a **second / eastern Wayfinder-type site**: an Ancient route-survey or cartographic station related to the Chapter 1 Wayfinder family but large enough to support a proper mandatory dungeon and archive/survey discovery.

Locked boundaries:
- it must be a real dungeon/adventure, not only an exposition room;
- its visual language is physical and archaeological: carved route tables, stone/metal survey surfaces, measuring marks/tools, repositories, old roadkeeping/survey architecture, collapsed galleries, physical records;
- do **not** present it with computer/network/database vocabulary;
- the exact world-map placement, route, site proper name, dungeon-room sequence, and mandatory boss identity/name remain **OPEN pending the user's next world-map pass and later encounter design**.

### 6.2 Final eastern survey

The Chapter 10 site preserves or leads to the final eastern survey needed to reconcile the accumulated cartography.

With it, Torren, Nimera, Vaelira, and the wider party can finally produce a coherent physical/geographic picture of Ancient Yahtrea from west to east and north to south.

The payoff is visual and spatial, not a premature lore explanation:
- roads, works, command routes, surveys, and buried approaches line up across enormous distances;
- some relationships continue beneath terrain where ordinary surface-road logic is insufficient;
- the complete picture shows that the sources describe parts of one much larger physical system/geography;
- the full picture has a significant unresolved relationship to the Crownhold / Caelora area.

They know **that the pieces belong together**. They still do **not** know **what the whole system truly was for**.

## 8. Othmar Calder — late payoff of the old-order mystery

The eventual initiating responsibility behind the Chapter 3 composite-order architecture traces to **Chancellor Othmar Calder**.

This does **not** mean Calder crudely forged the old source documents.

Locked interpretation:
- some source authorities are genuine old Crown records and are older than Calder's active investigation; some may ultimately be established as substantially older than Calder himself;
- the exact years/decades of those source documents remain **OPEN for a precise chronology pass**;
- Calder spent years researching old Crown records and Diysean archives, including suppressed/obsolete decrees, warrants, custody precedents, surveys, maps, and related material;
- Calder deliberately brought authentic but unrelated old authorities into contact in ways that could produce or justify new lawful-seeming directives;
- his method is therefore **real pieces, wrong assembly**, consistent with Chapter 3;
- his interest in this material predates Cyanis finding the Card.

Calder's archival behavior should be presented through physical and institutional evidence rather than modern computing metaphors.

Approved evidence vocabulary includes:
- archive withdrawal slips;
- old Crown requisition books and ledgers;
- transfer records and box/case inventories;
- authentic seals or retired seals;
- marginal notes and handwritten annotations;
- suppressed-stack references;
- old decrees, custody actions, warrants, surveys, and maps;
- Diysean tablets or copied translations bearing later scholarly/official annotations.

Avoid presentation as digital logs, access logs, databases, network requests, nodes, or hacking.
